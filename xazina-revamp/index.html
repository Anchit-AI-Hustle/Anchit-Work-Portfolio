DESIGN NOTESAbout this concept

How I built it.

An independent concept, not a commission. What I set out to fix, what I left alone, and how I would know whether it worked.

  1. 01 Problem

    Xazina’s catalog is deep: 436 products, 110 collections and 162 journal stories. Depth is hard to browse, and rebuilding the store from a snapshot showed me exactly where it strains.

    429 of the 436 products carry no product type, so a card had nothing to say about what kind of piece it was. The captured menu was flat, so For Her listed “Solitaire” twice, once for rings and once for earrings, with nothing to tell them apart.

  2. 02 What I kept

    Everything that is Xazina’s. The catalog, prices, variants, collections, journal stories and policies all come from one snapshot of xazina.com, with Xazina’s real product photography from its Shopify store.

    Xazina’s own home-page blocks stay, under “From the house.”, and so does its “Our most loved” list near the top. Purchases and appointments stay with Xazina.

  3. 03 What I changed

    • A new opening: a three-scene film and a Light Lab you can turn by hand, both made for this concept.
    • The menu groups pieces under Rings, Earrings, Wrists and Neck, so each “Solitaire” sits under its own category.
    • Every card names the piece’s most specific collection, such as “Cocktail Rings” or “Stud Earrings”, instead of one fallback label repeated on 429 cards.
    • Prices read one way. The capture mixed “₹293,617.00”, “₹ 148,756” and “₹2,93,617”; every price now uses Indian grouping and shows paise only when a price has them.
    • I removed what the capture leaked onto the page: Shopify’s hidden screen-reader labels for the regular, sale and unit price (and a stray “/per”), plus an empty “Type:” label before every product title.
    • In my first build, 35 collections whose pieces live in curated blocks opened on a count of zero pieces above an empty grid. They now open on the edit itself.
    • Search and share descriptions fit 60 to 160 characters. When my first build cut the full product story at 210, 422 product pages failed that check.

  4. 04 Hypothesis

    I think shoppers reach the right piece faster when every card says what it is and the menu says where it sits.

    I also think a bag that hands Xazina a filled cart carries more people through to checkout than a link that makes them find each piece again. Both are hypotheses; neither has been tested with real shoppers.

  5. 05 How I’d measure it

    Intended metrics. Not measured: this concept has no traffic.

    Product page to bag
    Share of product-page views that end in Add to bag.
    Search to product
    Share of searches followed by a product-page visit.
    Bag to Xazina hand-off
    Share of bag views that end in Continue to Xazina checkout.

  6. 06 What is real, what is a demo

    Real: the products, prices, variants, photos and copy, from a catalog snapshot of xazina.com. The build generates all 852 pages of this concept from that snapshot. The checkout is Xazina’s own cart.

    Mine: the film, the Light Lab and every layout. Prices are a snapshot, so they can drift from the live store, and Xazina confirms them at checkout. I am not affiliated with Xazina.

  7. 07 How checkout works here

    1. Add a piece on its product page. The bag is kept in this browser only.
    2. The bag lists your pieces at snapshot prices.
    3. “Continue to Xazina checkout” opens xazina.com/cart with the same pieces and quantities already in it.
    4. Xazina confirms price, stock, tax and delivery, and takes the payment.

    Unlike my Deli Chic and Gram Street Coffee concepts, where the order is a simulated demo, a purchase here really happens, on xazina.com.
