What is this case trying to prove?

The problem: Gaana's iPhone experience makes listening and buying harder than it should be. The opportunity: familiar Mirchi voices and Indian listening rituals might give people a reason to return. My approach: fix the basics, test one habit, then check that it helps listeners and the business.

My two-minute executive pitch

“I see three different customer decisions at Mirchi: a local advertiser buys credible reach and fulfilment, an event sponsor pays for a repeatable experience, and a Gaana listener pays for a better audio habit. I would measure each revenue stream after its direct costs, rather than claim that growing downloads or top-line sales creates profit.

My first Gaana priority would be trust. The supplied iOS screens show competing subscriptions above the music, inconsistent benefits and annual-price arithmetic that does not reconcile to the displayed terms. The mini-player obscures calls to action. A recurring paywall with a locked close button is reported; I would reproduce it and measure the effect, not assert that it already caused churn. The first release would fix these problems and establish first-play, D7, paid conversion, complaints and contribution baselines.

Then I would test the differentiated wedge. YouTube Music and Spotify are strong at catalogue, creator content and discovery. Mirchi owns culturally resonant voices and regional entertainment IP. A single opt-in AI RJ and one Indian daily ritual could give someone a reason to return, but only if listeners actually do and rights plus inference economics work. I would follow with social song dedications and fan communities only after that proof.

The North Star I would propose is weekly listeners who choose to listen meaningfully on multiple days. The economic gate is incremental contribution after licensing, acquisition, displaced ads and service. After 90 days I want a repaired experience, a statistically defensible habit experiment and a firm scale-or-stop recommendation.”

Why did Gaana lose ground to Spotify in India?

I would begin with the evidence. In YouGov’s urban survey, Spotify rose 18 percentage points between 2021 and 2024 while Gaana fell 10. Gaana removed free streaming in 2022. Spotify kept a free entry point and built Indian music discovery. Those choices plausibly changed who tried each product and who returned. To identify causes I would compare first-play and return rates before and after the paywall, split by language, acquisition source and prior listening. I would ask former listeners what replaced Gaana. See the four-service comparison

How would you bring FM and social listeners into Gaana?

I would pick one recurring city show and give each promotion a link to that episode. Say a short address on air. Use a tappable link on mobile social and a QR code at events. Preserve the episode through app opening or web fallback. Measure arrivals, successful first plays and seven-day return separately by source. I would charge the pilot for host time, production and displaced ad airtime before calling it low-cost.

How would you improve paid conversion and renewal?

At ET Prime I worked on the path from reader interest to paid subscription. At Gaana I would first separate visitors, trial users, bundled users and existing subscribers. Test a clear renewal date and a relevant second episode for bundle users. In a separate experiment test a ₹9–₹19 weekly pass against the existing offer. Compare additional contribution per eligible visitor after renewals, refunds and people switching down from a monthly plan. At VAHDAM I worked on purchase journeys and repeat demand. Both experiences point me toward measuring the second purchase as carefully as the first.

What would you build to compete on discovery?

I would test a one-tap city stream presented by a familiar host. Let listeners skip, save and resume. Start with one language and offer a low-data mode. Compare time to first audio, skips, repeat days and four-week retention against the existing journey. Test voice search with real accents and noisy environments before broad rollout. Further station and advertiser practice

What would you improve first?

I would pick one city and one recurring show. I would listen to several editions, speak to regular and occasional listeners, and sit with the producer and sales team. I would choose a problem only after seeing where listeners lose interest or advertisers struggle to renew.

How would you bring listeners back tomorrow?

Give them a reason to return at a specific time. For example, a morning host could invite commuters to nominate a troublesome junction, check the facts and report back the next day. I would track repeat contributors and use listener research to check whether people actually tuned back in.

How would you know a radio show is working?

Use the available city audience research for reach and listening time, with the survey date and sample clearly stated. Calls and messages tell us who participated. A small vocal group can love a segment even while the wider audience switches away.

How does Radio Mirchi earn money?

An advertiser pays for airtime or a package that can include a host mention, a local activity or sponsorship. For airtime, multiply the seconds sold by the average price earned per second. Ask sales about discounts and finance about direct delivery costs before claiming profit.

A café asks for a discount. What do you do?

Ask what the café wants customers to do. Offer a short campaign in the right neighbourhood and time slot, with a distinct booking code. A 5% price cut requires about 5.3% more seconds sold just to keep revenue level. Check capacity before promising it.

How would you show an advertiser that the campaign worked?

Agree on a measure before airing the ad. For a café, record bookings during comparable weeks, track a campaign code and ask about other promotions. Report spots aired, responses and estimated additional bookings separately.

How would you compete with another FM station?

Compare the same city, language, time slot and listener need. Listen to both shows and ask commuters what made them stay or switch. For advertisers, compare two proposals against the same brief, including price, host involvement, delivery and reporting.

How would you turn a popular segment into a podcast?

Start with one rights-cleared spoken segment. Give it a clear title, edit out broadcast-only material and link listeners to the next episode. Compare episode starts, completion and return. Confirm who owns publishing and distribution before committing to a channel.

How would you decide whether to repeat an event?

For that city and edition, add collected sponsor income and net ticket receipts, then subtract venue, talent, production and promotion costs. Check attendance, complaints, sponsor interest in returning and when cash arrives. A sold-out event can still lose money.

Which AI use would you test first?

I would test a tool that suggests short clips from an RJ interview. The producer approves the edit, context and rights. Compare minutes spent per publishable clip, including corrections, against the current process. Stop if review takes as long as manual editing.

How would you use web, mobile and social for a station?

Pick one task, such as finding a segment heard on air. Follow the path from a host mention or social post to the correct clip on a phone. Measure successful plays and repeat visits, and find who can fix each broken step.

Which revenue figures can you quote?

ENIL publishes group results. I would cite those with their period and label, then ask for the station or radio-business figures that fit this role. For my own work I would track ad revenue, price per second, repeat advertiser spend and direct costs within the agreed scope.

What would you do in 90 days?

In month one, observe a station and establish a baseline. In month two, pilot one change with a producer or a local sales lead. In month three, review the outcome and costs, then decide together whether to repeat it in a second city.

How do you measure engagement without confusing reach and a listening habit?

New eligible visitor to first audible song, meaningful repeat listening on days 1, 7 and 30, and weekly listeners active on at least two separate days. Track language, country, app version, source, device and subscription status. For Mirchi, track episode continuation, stream completion and migration; for Gaana, paid contribution after royalties, fees and displaced advertising. A store rating is not D7 retention.

Why not ship AI RJ immediately?

An AI voice has little value if the player blocks Subscribe or users cannot play songs. The proposed order is: stop coercive paywall patterns and broken UI, correct billing, establish baseline cohorts, then opt-in one licensed RJ voice and ritual with a comparable no-RJ control. Stop if habit or net contribution does not improve after rights and inference costs.

Prioritize based on task severity and affected population, not a single featured review. Do not claim a current widespread bug without evidence.

Defending original video series, age targeting and online watchstreams

Why a mini-OTT product when YouTube, Instagram and others already show short video?

Do not clone their distribution. Test converting rights-cleared Mirchi audio IP into characters and mini-series people return to, then let social feeds drive discovery. Build a standalone OTT product only if retention, monetization and rights require it. Until then, use a series shelf and owned episode navigation.

Mirchi already has AI audio stories. What is actually new?

The proposal is selective audio-to-video adaptation, stronger episodic direction, stable character identity, original songs and rights-cleared multi-format IP. Existing audio shows are inputs, not a gap in the business.

What age group should these stories target?

Current Gaana/Mirchi app age distributions are not disclosed. A Gaana 2020 media kit historically reported 39% aged 18–24, 30% 25–34 and 21% 35–44; these are not 2026 audience measurements. Test 18–34 for discovery and 25–44 for family/comedy, include 45+ and Unknown, then let episode return and net contribution decide.

Why would anyone switch for Mirchi Live?

They would not switch for passive streaming. Offer an RJ-hosted licensed premiere in which a moderated vote changes an approved scene, a dedication is featured with consent, or a city co-creates a credited original song. Test whether that distinct outcome improves voluntary return compared with the same content without participation.

Can a watchstream play existing songs across devices?

Only with the appropriate public-performance, music, audiovisual, territories, recording and synchronization rights. A consumer streaming license is insufficient for many shared uses. Establish legal/rights clearance before product promises.

What makes a mini-series or live event profitable?

Episode-one-to-two continuation, week-two voluntary return, live meaningful participation and incremental receipts after rights, creative talent, editing, AI inference, platforms, moderation, acquisition and refunds. No invented DAU, conversion percentage or cost assumption. Stop if safety, rights, playback or contribution fails.

Hard interviewer pushbacks and defensible answers

Why can’t Spotify or YouTube copy an AI RJ?

They can copy generated DJ speech. The possible advantage is a permissioned RJ relationship, local editorial judgment, Mirchi talent, festivals and community. I would compare the real RJ-assisted ritual with a good non-RJ playlist; without incremental weekly habit and profit, there is no moat.

How do you avoid cannibalising advertising revenue?

Incremental net subscription revenue plus retained renewal value minus displaced audio/display ads, royalty and app fees, partner share, service, CAC and TTS. Compare eligible cohorts and separate free heavy listeners from new paid buyers. Paid conversion can rise while total profit falls.

What if AI voice inference costs break the model?

Use rights-cleared recorded material and caching when sensible, script short interventions, offer opt-out, select a quality-safe low-cost model, cap per-user/weekly inference, and monitor cost per retained listener. Stop if unit contribution after rights and editorial review does not beat the control.

How would you quantify emotional connection?

Measure voluntary return to the same RJ/ritual on distinct days, successful dedications and qualified invitee retention, alongside opt-in qualitative interviews. Control for seasonality and notification prompting. Raw likes and session duration alone are not emotional connection.

Are ₹709 and ₹899 actually two different premium tiers?

Not necessarily. ₹799 is presented as a reference price in one offer, while the existing website describes a ₹899 one-time annual product. I would inspect SKU IDs, auto-renew, eligibility and receipts across iOS/web. The observed ₹709/month equivalent does not reconcile to either 12 or 13 months; correcting that is a P0 trust issue.

Did the paywall cause D7 retention to fall?

We do not know. The recurring locked-close behaviour is user-reported, not a measured experiment; I would reproduce it, stop coercive timing, then measure first-play, exits, D1/D7, paywall exposure, reviews and support complaints. I would never present a hypothetical uplift as actual research.

Only two engineers available. What ships?

Fix blocked mini-player navigation and remove forced modal re-entry. Connect display plan copy to one accurate offer schema and validate checkout and restore. These are fundamentals. AI RJ remains a small partner-owned test after the base experience works.

Why might Mehfil fail?

Licensed playback and synchronization restrictions, invite friction, spam, moderation and awkwardness. Start with a small rights-cleared private-room format, permissioned voice notes and visible report/block; judge invited users who play and return after safety costs.

Metric tree, A/B test and decision logic

Acquisition → Activation → Habit → Monetisation → Retention: qualified unique arrivals → successful first intentional play → 2+ distinct listening days in a week → eligible paid starts and net contribution after CAC/royalty/inference/ad displacement → D1/D7/D30 and paid renewals. Separate direct-paid, partner bundle and trial cohorts.

P0 experiment: stable user-level randomisation among eligible unpaid iOS listeners, stratify by channel and SKU. Variant removes locked-close timer, caps interruptions, presents an intent-triggered offer and fixes plan/accessibility clarity. If it is inappropriate to withhold a safety fix, ship it broadly and report observational evidence with limitations. Primary: intentional first play and D7; commercial: incremental contribution per eligible user; guardrails: refunds, exits, app errors, complaints and ad yield. Power from actual baseline. Isolate pricing text and bottom-overlay changes when feasible. Full methodology.
