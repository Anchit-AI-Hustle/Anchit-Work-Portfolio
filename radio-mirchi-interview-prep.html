My 90-second opening

“My background sits at the intersection of product, subscription growth and hands-on execution. I have worked on consumer journeys at Times Internet and D2C growth at VAHDAM, and I also build products myself. What interests me about Mirchi is the combination of local trust, creators, advertisers and live experiences.

From the latest ENIL results, I see a business diversifying while working hard on profitability. I would approach radio, events, digital solutions and Gaana as different economic engines, then identify where shared relationships and content can create repeat customer value.

I have looked at the work already underway, including Gaana product updates, distribution partnerships, regional content and live formats. My starting point would be to understand your mandate and data, then choose a focused customer or commercial journey, ship a bounded improvement and measure profit after direct costs as well as customer experience. I would like to bring both the analytical discipline and the ability to build and deliver that work.”

Practice questions and answer frameworks

Practise aloud: conclusion, evidence, decision, measurement.

How would you describe Mirchi’s business?

I see ENIL as a city-centric entertainment and advertiser company with radio as a foundation, alongside events, digital solutions and Gaana. I would understand each Income and cost sheet separately and use the shared assets, local relationships, creators and IP, to improve repeat customer value.

What stood out in the latest financial results?

Q2 FY27 consolidated operating revenue was ₹116.70 crore, down 17.3%, while consolidated EBITDA was ₹12.05 crore, up 5.9%. The domestic headline is different: ₹11.26 crore EBITDA, up 9.5%. I would read that as improving operating discipline in a weak revenue environment, while watching net losses and sustainable demand.

S1

What would you improve first?

I would confirm the mandate and starting result, then pick a bounded journey. Given existing Gaana distribution work, partner redemption to retained listening is a strong candidate. For a commercial remit, I would prioritise an advertiser repeat-spend or event economics workflow. I would not choose before seeing user group profit after direct costs and current commitments.

How would you grow event revenue?

Start by separating owned IP, commissioned work and promoted partner events. For each city edition, understand sponsor commitments, actual income per ticket after deductions, break-even attendance and cash advances. Grow the formats that earn repeat attendance and sponsor renewal at acceptable profit after direct costs and service quality.

Would you build a new Mirchi super-app?

I would first test whether the need can be served through existing Mirchi, Gaana and booking journeys. Another app introduces acquisition, paid access and maintenance work. I would invest in a new surface only if a validated cross-business job needs it and existing surfaces cannot serve it economically.

How would you evaluate the Noise partnership?

Separate potential reach, devices sold, eligible buyers, redemptions, activated listeners and renewals. A complimentary year is not a paid conversion. Compare profit from users who stay by user group after partner terms, content and support costs, and keep measuring beyond the first 90 days.

What is your view on radio’s future?

Radio can remain valuable where a city audience trusts its personalities and timely local context. I would improve the commercial proposition and delivery proof while protecting listener value. I would also recognise advertising pressure rather than assume the medium will recover automatically.

Where would you use AI?

I would observe content and campaign workflows, then test a specific bottleneck such as transcription, tagging or report preparation. I would measure usable turnaround time and correction burden, keep editorial and talent controls, and scale only if the quality-adjusted economics improve.

How do you prioritise without internal data?

Use public evidence to form hypotheses, not a confident backlog. Define the customer need, likely economic lever, dependency and cheapest validation. Replace provisional priority with measured reach, effort, starting result performance and profit after direct costs once inside the company.

What would you not claim from this study?

I have not measured native app playback, billing recovery, cancellations or Mirchi’s internal funnels. Store ratings are snapshots; old featured reviews are not a recent defect census. I would bring these as targeted research questions rather than claim they prove current user pain.

How does your experience connect?

My background connects subscription growth, consumer journeys and hands-on product delivery. ET work helps me reason about repeat paid value and conversion; D2C work helps with acquisition and lifecycle economics. I would bring that approach to Mirchi while learning its radio, event and rights-specific operating realities.

Why Mirchi?

Mirchi sits at an interesting intersection of people, local culture and commercial execution. The opportunity I find compelling is connecting that existing strength with better customer journeys and measurable economics. I would like to own a clear business outcome and build the smallest useful product that moves it.

How do you handle sales versus product priorities?

Agree the customer promise, delivery cost and success measure together. Distinguish a reusable product requirement from a one-client custom request. If custom work is commercially justified, price and staff it explicitly instead of hiding it inside the roadmap.

What is the biggest risk in diversification?

New revenue can carry lower margins, larger operational complexity or more cash exposure. I would look at extra profit after direct costs and capital requirements, and avoid celebrating a larger non-radio percentage just because the radio base number fell.

What would make you stop a pilot?

A failed customer need, no credible measurement, worse profit after direct costs, repeated service issues or an unready rights / billing dependency. I would document the learning and stop quickly enough to protect capacity and customer trust.

Questions to ask the interviewers

  • Which outcome does this role own directly, and which business is the priority?
  • Where do you see the biggest gap today: demand, product experience, earning money or operating execution?
  • How are Gaana partner user groups evaluated beyond redemption?
  • Which event formats deliver the most repeatable profit after direct costs and sponsor renewal?
  • How do city sales, central product and content teams share ownership of an integrated offer?
  • What is already on the roadmap that I should build on?
  • What would you want the person in this role to have changed by day 90?

Ten minutes before the interview

  • Remember ENIL versus Mirchi and domestic versus consolidated numbers.
  • Review the latest initiatives; acknowledge existing work before proposing a change.
  • Prepare two real stories: one growth result and one hard delivery / prioritisation decision.
  • Have the event and revenue and costs for each subscriber models ready to explain assumptions.
  • Use “my hypothesis” when you have not verified the problem.
  • Finish answers with how you would measure the outcome.

A reliable structure for my examples

  • Situation: what customer or business problem existed and how it was established.
  • Ownership: the decision I owned and the people I worked with.
  • Action: what changed and why other options were rejected.
  • Evidence: time period, base number, result and how causality was assessed.
  • Learning: what I would repeat, change or investigate next.
