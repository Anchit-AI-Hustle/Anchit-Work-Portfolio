Commercial follow-up

“At Times Internet, I worked on what brings a reader back. At VAHDAM, I worked on what turns interest into a purchase. What interests me about Mirchi is that both questions play out in the same city. A commuter chooses a familiar host each morning, and a local business decides whether advertising on that show was worth paying for.

I would start by sitting in on a show and a local sales meeting. Then I would pick one problem we can measure together, such as helping a returning advertiser see what its campaign delivered.”

Who is the audience, and who pays?

“I would start with the buyer’s objective. A café wants local people to remember its offer, so it can buy repeated ads on a city station. A car dealer needs people to experience a vehicle. Mirchi’s work with MG Hector shows how a host can take that audience from radio into a showroom. And with Dulux, the audience was the dealers themselves: Mirchi helped run a display contest and recognised stores on air.

I would judge each campaign by what the buyer wanted: bookings for the café, completed drives for the dealer, participating stores for Dulux. Then I would ask what it cost Mirchi to deliver and whether the buyer booked again.”

Mirchi Digital PM · 12 questions from the brief

How did you grow ET Prime Assisted Sales revenue from ₹15 lakh to ₹80 lakh per month?

I worked on two connected problems at ET. First, high-intent readers needed a way to ask for help with buying. Introducing assisted-buying entry points grew qualified leads from about 30 to over 150 a day. Later I redesigned lead journeys and call flows, ran A/B experiments and built call transcription, quality scoring and pitch assistance so the sales team could review calls consistently. Monthly assisted-sales revenue went from ₹15 lakh to ₹80 lakh in four months. The programme reported over 400% ROI within six months. Lead volume was an earlier result. The later revenue figure reflects the programme and the sales team together. To explain ROI I would use the actual attributable gains and programme costs from the project records.

Likely follow-ups Which funnel stages changed? What was the denominator? What was your individual contribution? How exactly was ROI computed?

Evidence to have ready Reconcile programme costs, collections and the exact attribution window.

How did you deliver 27% engagement growth and ₹3 crore+ incremental annual revenue at ET Markets?

At ET Markets I worked on the web and mobile-web experience. The reported results were 27% higher engagement and over ₹3 crore in incremental annual revenue across the ET Markets and ET rebuild work. The app had a separate set of outcomes: daily active users rose 25%, average session duration rose 15% and screen views rose 19%. Those measures answer different questions. Longer sessions can mean deeper reading or a harder journey. I would show the original engagement definition, audience and comparison window before attributing a change to the redesign. For Mirchi I would apply the same discipline to a play start, an actual listen and a listener who comes back.

Likely follow-ups What is engagement here? What period? What else could have driven revenue? Were the app metrics causal?

Evidence to have ready Bring the engagement formula, baseline dates and revenue calculation.

How would you improve retention for a Mirchi digital audio product?

I would start with one group: first-time listeners arriving from a host’s social post. Can they reach the promised episode, start listening and find the next one? I would measure time to first listen and return on days 1, 7 and 30 by language, platform and source. Then I would test a direct episode link and a clear next-episode action against today’s journey. Success is a higher share of people returning to listen. If reminder opens rise while listening holds flat or opt-outs rise, I would change the reminder or remove it. ET Markets gives me experience measuring repeat use, and Chatter gives me a reference for live, time-sensitive content.

Likely follow-ups What is the north-star metric? Which cohort first? What if notifications increase opens but reduce satisfaction?

Evidence to have ready Define a meaningful listen and the eligible return cohort before launch.

Mirchi DAU has declined 20% in a month. How would you diagnose it?

In this hypothetical decline, I would first check whether the daily active user count still measures the same action. I would compare analytics with playback logs, release dates, outages and comparable weekdays. Then I would split the fall into new, continuing and returning-after-a-break listeners. If monthly reach is steady, people may simply be listening on fewer days. If the change is concentrated on one Android version, I would reproduce a play on that build and compare it with unaffected versions. If playback is healthy but fewer first-time users arrive, I would investigate distribution. The first decision depends on where the loss begins.

Likely follow-ups What if MAU is flat? What if only Android is down? How do you distinguish a tracking break?

Evidence to have ready Show how independent playback logs distinguish tracking loss from real listener loss.

How would you choose a monetisation model for Mirchi Digital?

I would preserve free listening where reach creates advertiser value. For one show, I would model sellable ad impressions, the share sold, net price and delivery costs per listener. A premium benefit such as early access needs rights, repeat demand and a payment owner. I would first test interest with a clear offer, then compare paid conversion, cancellations, foregone ad income and content costs. An ad can earn money today and reduce future income if it drives regular listeners away. Times Health+ and ET Prime give me experience with paid propositions. I would test a Mirchi-owned offer or seek the distribution partner’s agreement before proposing a paywall.

Likely follow-ups How would you set pricing? When does an ad hurt LTV? What would you test before building a paywall?

Evidence to have ready Compare net income per eligible listener over the same period.

What would your first six-month product roadmap for Mirchi look like?

In month one I would agree on the business outcome, map a listener’s journey with producers and engineers, verify tracking and establish a baseline. In months two and three I would repair any reproducible playback failure and trial one improvement for a defined group. In months four to six I would extend a successful test to a second show or language, then test sponsorship or another paid benefit if the economics justify it. Each release would have an owner, an acceptance test and a rollback condition. If leadership needs an earlier launch, I would reduce the scope to one show while protecting playback reliability.

Likely follow-ups What if the CEO demands a feature? How would you allocate engineers? Which KPI comes first?

Evidence to have ready Name the owner and decision at the end of each phase.

Tell me about a conversion funnel you improved. What changed and why?

At ET Prime, my funnel ran from a high-intent reader seeing the assisted-buying option to requesting help, qualifying, speaking with sales and paying. I improved entry points and later redesigned call flows. The portfolio records roughly 30 to 150-plus qualified leads per day. That is volume. To assess conversion, I would divide qualified leads by eligible visitors, and paid buyers by qualified leads, for the same period and traffic mix. Sales follow-up speed and staffing can affect both. At VAHDAM I also worked on international D2C purchase journeys and lifecycle campaigns in Klaviyo and WebEngage. I would use the ET example for the quantified outcome.

Likely follow-ups Did conversion rise or did traffic rise? What was the control? How did sales operations affect outcomes?

Evidence to have ready Bring one actual test design and its denominator.

How could Mirchi differentiate itself from Spotify and YouTube?

Spotify and YouTube already offer convenient discovery. Mirchi has a reason to earn a return visit when someone wants a particular city host, a missed segment or participation in a local show. I would put a rights-cleared catch-up episode behind a direct mobile-web link from the host’s post. That lets a listener try it before deciding whether to install anything. I would compare first-play success and seven-day return against the current link. If people find the episode but rarely come back, I would test the programme and release schedule before investing in another app.

Likely follow-ups Why would users install another app? What is the distribution advantage? How do you test without licensing costs?

Evidence to have ready Confirm who can publish each clip and how a returning listener is counted.

Tell me about an experiment that failed or disproved your hypothesis.

One assumption I would challenge from my assisted-sales work is that more qualified leads automatically mean a better customer experience. The programme grew revenue, but sales-call complaints raised a different question: who actually wanted a call? My retrospective proposed limiting calls to people who asked and excluding existing subscribers. That proposal has no measured outcome. It is an example of changing my recommendation after reviewing evidence. For a controlled experiment that actually failed, I would use a specific project record showing the hypothesis, comparison, result and decision. The available record supports the retrospective only.

Likely follow-ups What was the sample size? Why was the test inconclusive? What did you stop doing?

Evidence to have ready A specific failed A/B test, sample size and observed result require your own project record.

How do you prioritise when engineering capacity is limited and stakeholders disagree?

I would ask sales, editorial and engineering to agree on the user outcome and deadline. Suppose a sponsor wants a personalised experience in three weeks and engineering estimates eight. I would propose a rights-cleared page for one host, with a direct play link and verified campaign reporting. I would defer personalisation and reserve engineering time for playback failures. I would write down the trade-off, the accountable decision maker and what evidence would reopen it. My ET work joining sales operations, product journeys and call tooling informs this approach. The sponsor negotiation is an illustrative decision.

Likely follow-ups What if two senior leaders disagree? What would you drop? When do you escalate?

Evidence to have ready Choose a real disagreement if asked for a historical incident.

Where would you apply AI to improve Mirchi’s consumer experience?

I would test natural-language discovery in a small rights-cleared archive. A listener might remember the host and topic but forget the episode title. I would compare ordinary keyword search with an AI-assisted search that returns playable episodes. Editors would judge relevance across languages and flag incorrect or unavailable results. I would measure successful listens, search time, cost per successful session and return listening. My Third Eye work involved retrieval and tool use, and TeleSuite involved reviewing machine-generated outputs. If better tags deliver the same result faster and more cheaply, I would ship the tags.

Likely follow-ups Why AI instead of rules? How do you evaluate recommendations? What about rights, privacy and latency?

Evidence to have ready Include incorrect results, rights, latency and cost in the release decision.

Further station and advertiser practice

What would you improve first?

I would pick one city and one recurring show. I would listen to several editions, speak to regular and occasional listeners, and sit with the producer and sales team. I would choose a problem only after seeing where listeners lose interest or advertisers struggle to renew.

How would you bring listeners back tomorrow?

Give them a reason to return at a specific time. For example, a morning host could invite commuters to nominate a troublesome junction, check the facts and report back the next day. I would track repeat contributors and use listener research to check whether people actually tuned back in.

How would you know a radio show is working?

Use the available city audience research for reach and listening time, with the survey date and sample clearly stated. Calls and messages tell us who participated. A small vocal group can love a segment even while the wider audience switches away.

How does Radio Mirchi earn money?

An advertiser pays for airtime or a package that can include a host mention, a local activity or sponsorship. For airtime, multiply the seconds sold by the average price earned per second. Ask sales about discounts and finance about direct delivery costs before claiming profit.

A café asks for a discount. What do you do?

Ask what the café wants customers to do. Offer a short campaign in the right neighbourhood and time slot, with a distinct booking code. A 5% price cut requires about 5.3% more seconds sold just to keep revenue level. Check capacity before promising it.

How would you show an advertiser that the campaign worked?

Agree on a measure before airing the ad. For a café, record bookings during comparable weeks, track a campaign code and ask about other promotions. Report spots aired, responses and estimated additional bookings separately.

How would you compete with another FM station?

Compare the same city, language, time slot and listener need. Listen to both shows and ask commuters what made them stay or switch. For advertisers, compare two proposals against the same brief, including price, host involvement, delivery and reporting.

How would you turn a popular segment into a podcast?

Start with one rights-cleared spoken segment. Give it a clear title, edit out broadcast-only material and link listeners to the next episode. Compare episode starts, completion and return. Confirm who owns publishing and distribution before committing to a channel.

How would you decide whether to repeat an event?

For that city and edition, add collected sponsor income and net ticket receipts, then subtract venue, talent, production and promotion costs. Check attendance, complaints, sponsor interest in returning and when cash arrives. A sold-out event can still lose money.

Which AI use would you test first?

I would test a tool that suggests short clips from an RJ interview. The producer approves the edit, context and rights. Compare minutes spent per publishable clip, including corrections, against the current process. Stop if review takes as long as manual editing.

How would you use web, mobile and social for a station?

Pick one task, such as finding a segment heard on air. Follow the path from a host mention or social post to the correct clip on a phone. Measure successful plays and repeat visits, and find who can fix each broken step.

Which revenue figures can you quote?

ENIL publishes group results. I would cite those with their period and label, then ask for the station or radio-business figures that fit this role. For my own work I would track ad revenue, price per second, repeat advertiser spend and direct costs within the agreed scope.

What would you do in 90 days?

In month one, observe a station and establish a baseline. In month two, pilot one change with a producer or a local sales lead. In month three, review the outcome and costs, then decide together whether to repeat it in a second city.

Why are you a fit?

At Times Internet I worked on why readers returned and paid. At VAHDAM I worked on how customers found and bought products. At Mirchi I would apply that experience to two concrete questions. What brings a listener back, and what earns the advertiser’s next booking?

What would you ask the panel?

Which cities and shows would I support? Which outcome would I own in my first quarter? Who decides programming, pricing, event commitments and publishing? What listener and advertiser information can the team actually use?
