More of the right leads: a property marketing engine

SPN Propservices LLP · Marketing Executive

Property is a long, high-stakes purchase. A sales team can only close leads that are a real fit, so the numbers that matter are qualified leads and conversions, not reach alone.

+20%Qualified property leads
+30%Engagement & reach in six months
+15%Conversions
10+Digital & offline campaigns supported

The challenge

In property, lead volume says little about lead quality. Buyers research for weeks, compare projects and talk to several brokers. Marketing that only chases reach sends sales teams after people who were never going to buy.

What she did

  • Supported 10+ digital and offline marketing campaigns
  • Managed the social media calendars
  • Conducted market and competitor analysis
  • Created conversion-focused marketing collateral for sales teams

The play

Read the market first

Market and competitor analysis supported a 15% improvement in conversions.

Digital and offline together

10+ campaigns across both, contributing to a 20% rise in qualified property leads.

Keep the feed on a calendar

Managed social media calendars grew engagement and reach by 30% within six months.

Arm the sales team

Conversion-focused collateral created for the people closing the deals.

The result

Qualified property leads rose 20%, with her campaign work contributing. Engagement and reach rose 30% in six months, and conversions improved 15%.

Why it matters for the role

She judges a funnel by its quality, and she builds for the sales team that has to close it.

2,000 tickets later: getting a CRM actually used

VOIS — Vodafone Intelligent Solutions · Senior Executive

A system nobody uses well is an expensive one. The work: keep users unblocked, check changes before they ship, and bring new hires up to speed fast.

2,000+User tickets resolved
200+Users & new hires trained
−20%Onboarding time
+25%Process efficiency

The challenge

Every release, new hire and process change is a chance for a CRM to lose its users. Untested changes break workflows, untrained people make workarounds, and broken processes slow everyone down.

What she did

  • Resolved 2,000+ user tickets
  • Supported UAT for 100+ user stories
  • Trained 200+ users and new hires
  • Improved process efficiency through stakeholder-led workflow changes

The play

Unblock the user

2,000+ tickets resolved. The queue is the closest view of where a system confuses people.

Test before it ships

UAT support on 100+ user stories before release.

Teach it properly

Training for 200+ users and new hires cut onboarding time by 20%.

Change it with the people who run it

Stakeholder-led workflow changes improved process efficiency by 25%.

The result

Onboarding was 20% faster and processes 25% more efficient. She also received Star of the Month twice.

Why it matters for the role

A CRM role is half change management. She has trained 200+ people on a system and changed processes with stakeholders.

The first 90 days as CRM & Lifecycle Marketing Manager

Operating plan · how she would run the role

A hiring manager wants to know what happens after the offer letter. This is her plan: audit what exists, fix what leaks, then grow what works, with the reason she can deliver each phase.

NoteThis is an operating plan, not a past result. Each phase lists the experience it rests on.

Days 1–30

Audit

  • Data health: duplicates, missing fields, untagged sources
  • Every live journey and automation listed, with broken ones fixed first
  • One funnel dashboard everyone agrees to use
  • A one-to-one with each stakeholder in sales, product and support

Why she can: 2,000+ tickets resolved and 50+ dashboards built. She knows where CRMs break.

Days 31–60

Build

  • A lifecycle map: welcome, activation, re-engagement, win-back
  • Segments by behaviour and stage, not only demographics
  • A testing plan that changes one variable per test
  • A written lead-handover agreement between marketing and sales

Why she can: she configured Sales and Service Cloud automation and supported 10+ campaigns.

Days 61–90

Scale

  • Holdout groups to prove each journey’s real, incremental lift
  • A monthly business review on pipeline and retention
  • Playbooks written down so the work outlasts the person
  • Team training on the new journeys and dashboards

Why she can: she trained 200+ people, cut onboarding time 20% and was part of 25% efficiency gains.

The scorecard she would report

  • Qualified pipeline created
  • Conversion rate at each funnel stage
  • Retention and repeat rate
  • Revenue influenced by lifecycle programmes

What it shows

She would arrive with a plan, report on the numbers that matter, and leave systems that keep running.
