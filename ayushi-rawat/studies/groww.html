Case studies/Groww

Brand case study 07 · Fintech · Activation & retention

42 lakh applied for an IPO in one quarter. The habit comes after.

In one quarter, 42 lakh people applied for IPOs through Groww and 11 lakh became new transacting users, “propelled by primary market activity”.1 Events bring people in; habits keep them. This study is Ayushi’s plan to turn an event-led first investment into a steady one, in a category where every message is governed by SEBI’s advertising rules.

The short version

The situation

Retention is the advantage

Groww added NSE active clients in a quarter when the industry lost them, and says strong retention “helped to more than offset” low new-user additions.2

The gap

Event-led first steps

New users often arrive with an IPO. Most users are outside the top six cities, and most women investing are first-timers.6 The second step is not yet a habit.

The play

Teach, then make it routine

An IPO-to-SIP bridge, a first-SIP onboarding for new investors, and calm, factual messages when markets fall. All written to SEBI’s code.

What Groww runs today

The rules: SEBI notified a common advertisement code for intermediaries, including stock brokers, on 8 Oct 2026. It bars “guaranteed or risk-free returns” and testimonials, bans dark patterns, and takes effect 180 days after publication.7 Under the exchange codes in force when SEBI consulted in June 2026, brokers needed prior exchange approval for advertisements, including app promotions.8

Where the value leaks

Four moments between a first step and a habit

01 · The event ends

An IPO is a moment, not a routine

Applying for an IPO is exciting and over in days. Without a next step that suits a beginner, the account goes quiet until the next one.

Evidence: fact 2. The drop-off after an IPO is Ayushi’s hypothesis; Groww does not publish it.

02 · First-time investors

Confidence before products

Most users are outside the big cities, and many women investing are doing it for the first time. Plain explanations earn trust that offers cannot.

Evidence: fact 8.

03 · The first dip

A falling market tests a new SIP

A new investor’s first downturn is when a SIP is most likely to be paused. Calm, factual information, and the control to decide, matter most then.

Groww does not publish SIP discontinuation; this is to be measured.

04 · The rules

Copy has to teach, not promise

No return claims, no testimonials, no nagging. A lifecycle programme built on hype would not survive the code; one built on clarity can.

Evidence: the rules note above.

How might we

…turn an event-led first investment into a steady habit, using only messages that inform rather than promise?

The plan

Three plays, each with a trigger, a message and a test

Play 1 · IPO to SIP

A next step that suits a beginner

After an IPO application closes, whatever the allotment, the user learns one thing at a time: what a mutual fund is, what a SIP is, how small a start can be. The ask is to explore, never to chase returns.

Trigger
First-ever transaction was an IPO application; no SIP on the account.
Sequence
Day 1: allotment explained. Day 5: “What is a SIP?” in two minutes. Day 14: an invitation to browse funds, with the risk disclaimer.
Channels
Email and in-app messages, only to users with recorded marketing consent and notifications allowed.
Compliance
No return figures, rankings or testimonials; every template goes through the approval route the rules require.
Sample copy · written for this study
Email · subjectday 5What is a SIP? Two minutes, no jargonHow a fixed monthly investment works, and what it doesn’t promise. Mutual Fund investments are subject to market risks, read all scheme related documents carefully.
App notificationday 14Explore funds at your own paceSee how SIPs work before you decide anything.
Play 2 · First SIP, first months

Make the first three months clear

A first SIP raises small questions: when is the debit, what is NAV, where are my units? Answer each one when it comes up, in the user’s chosen language, using only their own account facts.

Trigger
First SIP registered by a first-time investor.
Sequence
Day 0: what happens next. Two days before the first debit: a reminder. After it: “your units have arrived”, and how to read them. Month 3: a summary of what they have invested, with no performance claims.
Language
The app language the user chose, tested against English.
Stop rule
Transactional reminders only after month 3, unless the user asks for more.
Sample copy · written for this study
App notification2 days beforeYour first SIP debit is on the 5th₹500 from your linked account. You can change or pause it anytime.
App notificationafter debitYour units have arrivedHere’s how to read units and NAV, in one screen.
Play 3 · Steady in a dip

Information and control, not pressure

When markets fall sharply, SIP holders in their first year get a factual note: what changed, where their SIP stands, and that pausing or stopping is their choice and how to do it. No prediction, no urging.

Trigger
A broad index falls past a set threshold, for SIP holders in their first 12 months.
Content
What happened, their next debit date, a plain explainer of how SIPs work, with no reference to past returns, and the controls.
Frequency
At most one such note a month.
Review
Copy pre-approved with compliance before the first use, then reused.
Sample copy · written for this study
App notificationafter a fallMarkets fell this week. Here’s where your SIP standsYour next debit is on the 5th. You stay in control: change, pause or stop anytime.

How she would prove it

Measured against a holdout, not a hunch

North-star metricShare of new transacting users with an active SIP at day 90The step from an event to a routine.
Per play

Primary

  • First SIP within 30 days of an IPO-first account (Play 1)
  • SIPs still active after three debits (Play 2)
  • SIP continuation through a dip vs holdout (Play 3)
Guardrails

Must not get worse

  • Complaints and opt-outs
  • Every template approved before use
  • No first-year investor pushed toward derivatives
Test design

Decision rule

  • A random 10% holdout per journey
  • Read by cohort and city tier
  • Ship only if the lift beats the holdout and no guardrail breaks

Rollout

Ninety days, compliance in the loop

Days 1–30

Measure and clear

  • Second-step rates for IPO-first accounts
  • SIP status at day 90 by cohort
  • Every template through approval
Days 31–60

Launch Plays 1 and 2

  • The next big IPO’s first-time applicants
  • Two languages beyond English
  • Weekly read-out against the holdout
Days 61–90

Ready Play 3

  • Dip copy approved and on the shelf
  • Threshold and audience agreed with compliance
  • A playbook for each new code requirement

What could be wrong

Riskiest assumptions, cheapest tests

AssumptionWhy it might failCheapest test
IPO applicants want to learn about SIPsMany may only want the next IPO.Two cells: SIP explainer vs. upcoming-IPO calendar; compare second steps.
Plain education earns trustUsers may skim and ignore it.Track read-through and replies; shorten what is skipped.
A dip note helps, not alarmsA message about a fall can itself prompt a pause.Holdout on the first dip; read pause rates both ways before scaling.
Approval won’t slow the programmePre-approval adds time to every template.Build a library of approved templates before launch.

Consent and data: Groww’s privacy policy says it may send promotional emails with the ability to opt out.9 It does not name push notifications as a marketing channel, so in-app messages here go only to users who have allowed notifications, and all marketing relies on recorded consent as India’s DPDP Act, 2023 requires. Live versions of every message would carry the entity name, SEBI registration number and a link to the disclaimers, as the new code requires.7
