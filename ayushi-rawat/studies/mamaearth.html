Case studies/Mamaearth

Brand case study 04 · D2C personal care · Repeat purchase

A third of revenue goes on ads. A second order doesn’t need one.

Honasa Consumer, the company behind Mamaearth, spent 32.9% of FY26 revenue on advertising. It says D2C “acts as our listening post” and has passed 20 million D2C customers. This study is Ayushi’s plan to turn first orders on mamaearth.in into a routine, using the regimen kits and MamaCash wallet the brand already has, so each repeat order needs less paid media.

The short version

The situation

Growth is back, ads are heavy

Mamaearth accelerated to high-teens growth in Q1 FY27, after the 2024 distribution correction. Advertising is still 31.8% of revenue.

The gap

Promotions sell; routines retain

The site leads with Buy 1 Get 1 and bundle discounts. Regimen kits exist, but no subscription or replenishment programme is shown.

The play

Build the second order into the first

A regimen journey after each first order, MamaCash as the reason to come back, and referral asked at the right moment.

Where the value leaks

Four gaps between first order and habit

01 · Paid repeat

Buying the same customer twice

When advertising is about a third of revenue, a repeat order that arrives through a paid ad pays for that customer again. An email, push or WhatsApp to a known buyer avoids paying for that reach again.

Evidence: fact 2.

02 · Discount habit

Offers teach people to wait

Buy 1 Get 1 and stacked discounts win the first basket. Used on every visit, they train buyers to wait for the next offer before buying again.

Evidence: fact 6. Ayushi’s reading, to be tested.

03 · Routine

A concern is a regimen, not a product

Hairfall, acne and tan are the brand’s own kit categories. A buyer who starts with one product has the next step of the regimen waiting.

Evidence: fact 6.

04 · Listening post

The data loop needs questions

D2C is the brand’s first-party data source. A post-purchase check-in turns each order into a signal: did it work, what concern comes next.

Evidence: fact 4.

How might we

…make the second order on mamaearth.in feel like the next step of a routine, not the next discount?

The plan

Three plays, each with a trigger, a message and a test

Play 1 · Regimen journey

From one product to a routine

After a first order, map the product to its concern and send a short, useful sequence: how to use it, a check-in, then the regimen step that pairs with it. The kit is offered as the convenience, not as a discount.

Trigger
First order delivered, keyed to the concern of the product bought.
Sequence
Day 1: how to use it. Day 10: “How’s it going?” Before the pack is likely to run out: the refill and the next regimen step.
Channels
Email for the how-to, WhatsApp for opted-in buyers, push for app users.
Stop rule
Stop on repeat purchase. A poor check-in answer goes to care, not to selling.
Sample copy · written for this study
Email · subjectday 1Getting the most from your new hair oilHow often, how much and how long to leave it in. A short how-to inside.
WhatsApp · opted inday 10Ten days in, how’s your hair feeling?Reply 1 for better, 2 for no change, 3 for not good. We read every reply.
Play 2 · MamaCash

A wallet balance beats a blanket discount

Some buyers already hold MamaCash, for example from a referral. Remind them of their own balance before offering anything new. It gives them a reason to return without cutting the price for everyone.

Trigger
MamaCash balance above a set amount, and no order in 45 days.
Message
The balance in rupees, plus the product they are most likely to need now.
Promo rule
Site-wide offers are not sent to buyers in this journey, so the test compares wallet against discount.
Stop rule
Two reminders, then rest for 30 days.
Sample copy · written for this study
Pushday 45You have ₹150 in MamaCash 💚Enough for most of your next face wash. Tap to use it.
Email · subjectday 52Your ₹150 is still waitingHere’s what pairs with the sunscreen you bought in March.
Play 3 · Referral at the high point

Ask for the referral when they’re happiest

The ₹100 + ₹100 offer already exists. Instead of a standing banner, ask buyers at the moment they are most likely to recommend: right after a “better” check-in reply, or after their second order arrives.

Trigger
A positive check-in reply (Play 1), or second order delivered.
Message
One personal link, with the friend’s ₹100 explained in one line.
Fraud guard
The referrer is credited only after the friend’s first order is delivered, as the current terms already say.
Stop rule
One ask per buyer per quarter.
Sample copy · written for this study
WhatsApp · after a “better” replyday 11So glad it’s working 🙌Know someone dealing with hairfall too? Send them ₹100 off, and you get ₹100 when their order arrives.

How she would prove it

Measured against a holdout, not a hunch

North-star metricSecond-order rate within 60 days of a first D2C orderThe first step from a customer to a habit, measured by monthly cohort.
Per play

Primary

  • Repeat rate vs holdout (Play 1)
  • Reactivation per rupee of MamaCash vs discount (Play 2)
  • New buyers from referral (Play 3)
Guardrails

Must not get worse

  • Discount cost per repeat order
  • Unsubscribes and WhatsApp blocks
  • Negative check-in replies left unanswered
Test design

Decision rule

  • A random 10% of first-time buyers in a holdout
  • Read at 60 and 90 days, by cohort
  • Ship only if repeat beats the holdout and discount cost falls or holds

Rollout

Ninety days, one concern at a time

Days 1–30

Measure

  • Second-order rate by first product and concern
  • Repeat interval by pack size
  • How many buyers hold MamaCash, and how much
Days 31–60

Launch Play 1, hairfall only

  • One concern, one regimen, one clear read
  • Check-in replies routed to care
  • Weekly cohort read-out
Days 61–90

Add Plays 2 and 3

  • Extend Play 1 to acne and tan
  • Wallet vs discount test live
  • Check-in themes shared with the brand team

What could be wrong

Riskiest assumptions, cheapest tests

AssumptionWhy it might failCheapest test
D2C buyers come back to D2CThey may reorder on a marketplace or quick-commerce app, out of view.Ask at check-in: “Where will you buy your refill?” Count it as a signal, not a sale.
Buyers want a regimenMany may want one product that works, not a routine.Two cells: regimen message vs. single-product refill message.
Wallet reminders work without a discountSmall balances may not move anyone.Test balance thresholds; find the smallest balance that changes behaviour.
Check-ins won’t annoyA WhatsApp question can feel intrusive.Send only to opted-in buyers, measure blocks, and stop at the first sign of harm.

Consent first: WhatsApp only with opt-in under Meta’s business policy, and TRAI rules for SMS. Personal data is handled in line with India’s DPDP Act, 2023.
