Brand case study 01 · Beauty e-commerce · Loyalty & lifecycle

60 million have bought. 20 million bought this year.

Nykaa’s management says about 60 million people have ever bought from it, and about 20 million bought in the last 12 months. They also say purchase frequency is low against global peers. This study is Ayushi’s lifecycle plan for that gap, built on the Nykaa Privé loyalty programme the brand already runs.

IndependentAn independent case study, written to show how Ayushi would approach the problem. Not affiliated with, commissioned by or reviewed by Nykaa (FSN E-Commerce Ventures Ltd). Brand facts come from public sources, numbered and linked at the end. The plays, copy and test plans are hers. No results are claimed.

The short version

The situation

A huge base that buys rarely

By management’s own figures, about 40 million past buyers did not buy in the last 12 months. Those who do buy placed about 3.3 Beauty orders each in FY26.

The gap

Loyalty that waits to be found

Privé tiers are earned on rolling-year spend. The public terms describe status shown on a Loyalty page, with upgrades confirmed by email.

The play

Make the next order obvious

Three triggered journeys, each with a holdout: tier progress, routine replenishment and a value-first win-back.

What Nykaa runs today

Where the value leaks

Four gaps a lifecycle team can close

01 · Dormancy

Two in three past buyers are quiet

Every one of them already has an account and a purchase history. Reactivating a known buyer avoids paying to find them again, and it is a lever management named on the August 2026 call.

Evidence: fact 3.

02 · Tier distance

Gold is about 2.4 average baskets a year

₹5,000 at a ₹2,068 average basket is roughly 2.4 orders, and the average active Beauty buyer placed about 3.3 in FY26. Many buyers may be closer to a tier than they realise. Qualifying spend excludes GST and returns, so the true distance needs member data.

Evidence: facts 2, 4 and 6. ₹5,000 ÷ ₹2,068 ≈ 2.4.

03 · Routine

Beauty is a replenishment category

Sunscreen, cleanser and shampoo run out on a rhythm. A reminder timed to a buyer’s own repeat interval is useful rather than noisy.

Evidence: fact 7 names frequency as the gap.

04 · Spend mix

Acquisition is getting the pedal

Marketing is 15.3% of revenue, and management said it is pushing on acquisition. Retention work that lifts orders per buyer makes every rupee spent on acquisition earn more.

Evidence: fact 5 and the Q1 FY27 call.

How might we

…turn Privé from a reward people discover into a reason to place the next order, without training buyers to wait for a discount?

The plan

Three journeys, each with a trigger, a message and a test

Play 1 · Tier progress

“One order from Gold”

Find buyers whose rolling-year spend sits within one average basket of the next tier. Tell them exactly how far they are, and what the tier is worth, using benefits the programme already offers.

Trigger
Rolling spend within ₹2,100 of Gold or Platinum, and no order in 21 days.
Channels
App push, then email at 48 hours. WhatsApp only for buyers who opted in.
Offer
None at first. The tier is the incentive: free shipping, a birthday gift, a 2× birthday multiplier.
Stop rule
Two messages per cycle. Stop at once on purchase or opt-out.
Sample copy · written for this study
PushnowYou’re ₹1,640 from Privé Gold ✨One more order unlocks free shipping all year and a birthday gift.
Email · subject+48hYour Gold status is one basket awayHere’s what’s in your routine that’s due for a refill.
Play 2 · Routine

The refill that arrives before you run out

Learn each product’s typical repeat interval from order history, then remind each buyer at their own interval, not a calendar blast. Where Nykaa Now delivers, offer “today” as the convenience, not a coupon.

Trigger
Days since last purchase of a replenishable item reach that SKU’s median repeat interval for similar buyers.
Channels
Push first. Email carries the full routine, including the next step in the regimen.
Offer
Points on the reorder are already earned. Add a review prompt worth 1,000 points after delivery.
Stop rule
One reminder per item per interval. Suppress if the item was bought in-store.
Sample copy · written for this study
Pushday 38Running low on your SPF 50?Most people reorder about now. In your city it can be at your door in an hour.
Push · after deliveryday 3How’s it working for you?Review it and earn 1,000 Privé points. Add a photo for 2,000.
Play 3 · Win-back

A ladder that leads with value, not discount

Lapsed buyers get three steps. Step one reminds them what they already have: points, tier history, their usual brands. A discount is the last rung, and only for buyers who ignored the first two.

Trigger
No order in 120 days, among buyers with two or more past orders. A separate track handles single-order buyers.
Ladder
Day 0: points and favourites. Day 10: what’s new in their brands. Day 21: one capped offer.
Channels
Email and push, with WhatsApp on the final step for opted-in buyers only.
Stop rule
Exit after step three. Rest for 60 days before any further contact.
Sample copy · written for this study
Email · subjectday 0Your Privé points are still herePlus the three brands you buy most, all restocked.
WhatsApp · opted inday 21A welcome back, just for youFree shipping on your next order this week. Reply STOP to opt out.

How she would prove it

Measured against a holdout, not a hunch

North-star metricOrders per existing buyer per 12 monthsManagement’s own lever: frequency among people who already buy.
Per journey

Primary

  • Incremental orders per user in 60 days, vs holdout
  • Tier upgrades (Play 1)
  • Reactivation rate (Play 3)
Guardrails

Must not get worse

  • Push opt-outs and email unsubscribes
  • Discount cost per incremental order
  • Return rate on reactivated orders
Test design

Decision rule

  • A random 10% holdout per journey
  • Sample size set before launch
  • Ship only if the lift beats the holdout and no guardrail breaks

Rollout

Ninety days, one journey at a time

Days 1–30

Measure

  • Repeat intervals by SKU and category
  • Tier distance for every Privé member
  • Baseline frequency and opt-out dashboards
Days 31–60

Launch Play 1

  • The smallest journey, the clearest metric
  • UAT on every trigger before go-live
  • Weekly read-out against the holdout
Days 61–90

Add Plays 2 and 3

  • Only after Play 1 clears its decision rule
  • Shared frequency cap across all three
  • Playbook written for the CRM team

What could be wrong

Riskiest assumptions, cheapest tests

AssumptionWhy it might failCheapest test
Members care about tier statusMany may not know Privé exists, or may value price over perks.A two-cell push test: tier message vs. a plain reminder, same audience.
Repeat intervals are predictableBuyers mix online, store and other retailers, so history is partial.Back-test the predicted refill date against actual reorders from last year.
Value-first win-back worksLapsed buyers may only respond to price.Hold out step one for half the audience and compare reactivation.
More messages won’t annoyFatigue raises opt-outs and shrinks the reachable base. India’s 2023 dark-patterns guidelines also list “nagging”.5A shared frequency cap, with opt-outs as a stop-ship guardrail.

Consent and data: Nykaa’s privacy policy says it may contact customers by SMS, WhatsApp, phone, email and chat.4 This plan goes further and messages only buyers who have opted in to that channel. Personal data is handled in line with India’s DPDP Act, 2023.
