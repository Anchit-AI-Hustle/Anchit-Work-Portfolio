Case studies/Zomato

Brand case study 02 · Food delivery · Membership & notifications

The member who leaves at renewal.

In February 2024, Zomato told shareholders that customers were switching platforms when their Gold membership comes up for renewal, depending on who offers the lowest price. This study is Ayushi’s plan to win that moment with proof of value instead of a deeper discount, and to keep Zomato’s famous notification voice useful as it scales.

The short version

The situation

Growth from ordering more often

28 million monthly transacting customers, up 16%. Zomato says recent growth comes from more people ordering, and ordering more often.

The gap

Renewal is a price fight

Zomato has said Gold was used to acquire and re-acquire customers, and that at renewal many compared prices across apps (Feb 2024).

The play

Show members their own value

A personal renewal recap, an onboarding run for first-time online orderers, and a notification system with a brief and a cap.

What Zomato runs today

Where the value leaks

Three moments a lifecycle team owns

01 · Renewal

The member forgets what Gold saved them

If the only number at renewal is the price, the cheapest app wins. A member’s own record (orders placed, delivery fees avoided) is a number no rival can show them.

Evidence: fact 5 (Feb 2024).

02 · Opens vs orders

More visits, steady frequency

App opens rose while order frequency held steady. Some sessions end without an order, and that gap is where a well-timed, relevant nudge earns its place.

Evidence: fact 4. Ayushi’s reading, to be checked in the data.

03 · First orders

New users are new to ordering online

A first-time online orderer in a smaller city needs reassurance before habit: live tracking, refunds, the right price point. Ayushi’s working assumption: their first three orders decide whether they stay.

Evidence: fact 2.

04 · Notification load

A famous voice is easy to overuse

The tone works because it feels personal. Sent too often, it becomes noise and drives opt-outs. India’s dark-patterns guidelines name “nagging” as a pattern.

Evidence: facts 8 and the CCPA guidelines.8

How might we

…make a Gold member’s renewal decision about the value they have already had, not the lowest price on the day?

The plan

Three plays, each with a trigger, a message and a test

Play 1 · Renewal

“Your Gold year, in numbers”

Fourteen days before a membership lapses, show each member their own year: orders placed, delivery fees saved, favourite restaurants. Price comes after proof. A discount is the last step, and only for members who did not renew on value.

Trigger
Membership expiring in 14 days. A second touch on the expiry day.
Channels
An in-app card and push, then email with the full recap.
Segments
Heavy users get the recap only. Light users also get the cost of their usual order without Gold.
Stop rule
Stop on renewal. No more than three renewal messages in total.
Sample copy · written for this study
Pushday −14Your Gold year: 46 orders, ₹1,380 in delivery fees you didn’t payRenew before the 9th and keep it going.
In-app cardday 0Gold ends tonight 🌙Your biryani spot still delivers free with Gold. Tap to renew.
Play 2 · First three orders

Onboarding for people new to ordering online

For a user whose first-ever online food order was on Zomato, the job is trust, then habit. Each message answers one worry and suggests one easy next order at the price points the brand already promotes.

Trigger
First order delivered. Then order two and order three.
Content
Order 1: live tracking and how refunds work. Order 2: meals under ₹250 near them. Order 3: what Gold adds.
Language
The language the user set in the app, tested against English.
Stop rule
The journey ends at the third order or 30 days, whichever comes first.
Sample copy · written for this study
Pushafter order 1First one done ✅If anything is ever wrong with an order, refunds are a tap away in Help.
Pushday 4Dinner under ₹250, 20 minutes awayThree places near you that people keep reordering from.
Play 3 · The notification system

Humour with a brief and a cap

Keep the voice, and give every push a job. Each message has a trigger, a single action and a reason it is relevant to this person now. A shared cap protects the channel from every team at once.

Brief
Every push states its trigger, audience, one action and a fallback line for users who did not see the joke’s context.
Cap
A per-user daily and weekly limit shared across teams, with transactional updates exempt.
Quiet hours
No promotional pushes at night. Meal-time windows are learned per user.
Kill switch
Any message whose opt-out rate exceeds the channel average is paused automatically.
Sample copy · written for this study
Push · rain in your area7:40 pmRain outside, chai and pakoras inside ☔Your usual spot is open and 18 minutes away.
Push · cart left 25 min9:05 pmYour paneer tikka is still in the cartIt hasn’t gone anywhere. Neither has your appetite.

How she would prove it

Measured against a holdout, not a hunch

North-star metricGold renewal rate, at the same or lower discount costRetention that has to be bought with a deeper discount is not retention.
Per play

Primary

  • Renewal rate vs holdout (Play 1)
  • Share of new users reaching a third order in 30 days (Play 2)
  • Orders per push sent (Play 3)
Guardrails

Must not get worse

  • Push opt-out and app uninstall rate
  • Discount spend per renewal
  • Support contacts about notifications
Test design

Decision rule

  • A random holdout gets no recap, only the standard reminder
  • Sample size set before launch
  • Ship only if renewals beat the holdout and no guardrail breaks

Rollout

Ninety days, renewal first

Days 1–30

Measure

  • Renewal rate by member segment
  • Push volume per user, across all teams
  • Opt-out baseline by message type
Days 31–60

Launch Play 1

  • The renewal recap, with a holdout
  • The shared cap agreed and switched on
  • UAT on the savings calculation
Days 61–90

Add Play 2

  • Onboarding in two cities first
  • A brief template rolled out to every team
  • Monthly read-out on renewals and opt-outs

What could be wrong

Riskiest assumptions, cheapest tests

AssumptionWhy it might failCheapest test
Seeing their savings changes the decisionPrice-led members may renew on whichever app is cheapest, whatever the recap says.A recap vs. no-recap split on one month’s renewals.
Light users have savings worth showingA member with three orders a year may have saved little, and the recap could backfire.Show the recap only above a savings threshold; test the threshold.
First-time users want reassuranceThey may simply want the cheapest meal.Two cells: trust message first vs. price message first.
A cap won’t cost ordersSome high-intent pushes will be suppressed.Run the cap on half the base for four weeks and compare orders per user.

Zomato told the Central Consumer Protection Authority in 2025 that it is free of dark patterns, after a self-audit under the 2023 guidelines.9 A frequency cap and a kill switch keep a growing push programme consistent with that.
