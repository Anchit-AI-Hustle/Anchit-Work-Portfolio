Case studies/Myntra

Brand case study 05 · Fashion e-commerce · Repeat purchase

Won in the sale. Kept between them.

Myntra’s big sales keep bringing in first-time shoppers: 1.3X more in its June 2026 sale, 55% of them from non-metros.6 Its repeat customers grew 25% in 2025.3 This study is Ayushi’s plan for the weeks between sales: turning a first order placed during a festival into a second one placed at full price, using the fit and personalisation data Myntra already has.

The short version

The situation

Sales recruit, at a price

First-time shoppers grew 1.3X in the June 2026 sale, mostly from non-metros. Advertising and promotion cost ₹2,326.5 Cr in FY26, a year Myntra ended with a net loss.62

The gap

The weeks between sales

A shopper won by a festival discount has every reason to wait for the next one. The order that matters is the second, at full price.

The play

Style, fit and the right moment

A styling journey from the first purchase, a fit-feedback loop that makes the next size certain, and occasion triggers where M-Now can deliver tonight.

What Myntra runs today

The facts this plan is built on

Revenue from operations in FY26. Revenue excluding logistics rose 12% to ₹4,123.3 Cr; the fall overall came from logistics revenue.1

Where the value leaks

Four gaps between one sale and the next

01 · Sale-trained buyers

The discount taught them when to come back

A shopper whose first order was at festival prices has learned that waiting pays. Without a reason to return sooner, the next order waits for the next sale.

Evidence: facts 5 and 6. The behaviour is Ayushi’s hypothesis, to be read from cohort data.

02 · Paying twice

Every sale re-buys the same audience

Promotion spend is large against revenue. A second order earned through owned channels costs a fraction of one bought again with media.

Evidence: fact 4 and the arithmetic above.

03 · Fit doubt

Uncertainty about size stops the next order

Size recommendations already cover most apparel. Asking each buyer how the last piece fitted turns that model into a promise for their next one.

Evidence: fact 8. Myntra does not publish return rates, so none is assumed.

04 · The occasion

“I need it tonight” is a lifecycle moment

Where M-Now delivers, a wedding, a festival or a weekend plan is a reason to buy now, at full price, that a sale calendar cannot create.

Evidence: fact 7.

How might we

…turn a first order placed in a sale into a second order placed before the next one, without another discount?

The plan

Three plays, each with a trigger, a message and a test

Play 1 · Style it again

The first order becomes the first look

Once a sale order is kept, past the return window, the shopper gets ways to wear what they bought: three pieces that go with it, in their size, at full price. It sells the wardrobe, not the discount.

Trigger
First order placed during a sale event, kept after the return window closes.
Sequence
Day 0: “3 ways to wear it.” Day 14: new arrivals from the same brands, in their size. Day 30: one capped offer, only for shoppers who ignored both.
Channels
App push and email; any messaging channel only with that shopper’s opt-in.
Stop rule
Stop on purchase. Pause the journey two weeks before a sale event starts.
Sample copy · written for this study
Pushday 0That linen shirt? Three ways to wear it 👔Office, brunch and a wedding sangeet. Tap to see the looks.
Email · subjectday 14New in from the brand you bought, in your sizePicked to go with what’s already in your wardrobe.
Play 2 · Fit, settled

One question that makes the next size certain

A week after delivery, ask one thing: how did it fit? The answer goes into the shopper’s profile and the size recommendation, so the next product page can say “your size in this brand is M” with confidence.

Trigger
Apparel or footwear delivered and not returned after 7 days.
Question
Three taps: runs small, true to size, runs large. Optional: which size would you choose next time?
Use
The answer appears on the next product page from that brand, and in the next styling message.
Stop rule
One question per order, and at most two a month per shopper.
Sample copy · written for this study
Pushday 7Quick one: how did the jeans fit?Small · Just right · Large. We’ll remember for next time.
Product pagenext visitYour size in this brand: 32Based on the jeans you kept in October.
Play 3 · The occasion

“Tonight” is worth more than “20% off”

In cities where M-Now delivers, an occasion is a full-price reason to buy. When a shopper browses festive or party wear and has an item saved that M-Now can bring today, tell them it can arrive in time.

Trigger
Occasion browsing (festive, wedding, party) plus a wishlisted item in stock for M-Now delivery in their city.
Timing
Same day, before the M-Now cut-off. Never at night.
Offer
None. The value is the delivery time.
Stop rule
One occasion message a week; none in the 48 hours before a sale.
Sample copy · written for this study
Push4:10 pmThe kurta you saved can be there by 7 ✨In time for tonight. In your size, in stock near you.

How she would prove it

Measured against a holdout, not a hunch

North-star metricSecond orders at full price before the next sale, per 100 sale-acquired first-time shoppersRetention bought with the next discount does not count.
Per play

Primary

  • Second-order rate within 45 days (Play 1)
  • Fit answers per delivered order (Play 2)
  • Orders per occasion message sent (Play 3)
Guardrails

Must not get worse

  • Return rate on second orders
  • Push opt-outs and email unsubscribes
  • Share of second orders that needed an offer
Test design

Decision rule

  • A random 10% of each sale’s first-time shoppers held out
  • Read by sale cohort at 30 and 60 days
  • Ship only if full-price second orders beat the holdout

Rollout

Ninety days, one sale cohort at a time

Days 1–30

Measure

  • Second-order rate by sale cohort and city tier
  • Share of second orders bought at full price
  • Fit-related returns, by category
Days 31–60

Launch Plays 1 and 2

  • The latest festival cohort, with a holdout
  • Fit question on apparel first
  • Weekly cohort read-out
Days 61–90

Add Play 3

  • Two M-Now cities first
  • Fit answers shown on product pages
  • Playbook for the next sale’s cohort

What could be wrong

Riskiest assumptions, cheapest tests

AssumptionWhy it might failCheapest test
Sale shoppers can be moved to full priceSome buy only on discount, whatever they are shown.Compare full-price second orders: styling journey vs. no message, one cohort.
Shoppers will answer a fit questionResponse rates on post-purchase questions can be low.Send to 5% of deliveries for two weeks and measure answers.
Fit answers improve the next orderFit varies within a brand by line and fabric.Show “your size” on half of next-visit product pages; compare returns.
Occasions beat offersShoppers in non-metros may value price over speed.Split by city tier and read separately before scaling.

Consent first: messages only on channels a shopper has opted in to, TRAI rules for SMS, personal data handled in line with India’s DPDP Act, 2023, and no repeated nudging, which India’s 2023 dark-patterns guidelines list as “nagging”.8
