Proposed system · Vision Recall Engine

Every eye test starts a clock. This keeps time for the customer.

Lenskart already runs free eye tests, home and remote optometry, Free Lens Replacement, Gold and an AI stylist. The Vision Recall Engine connects them: it knows when each person's next eye test is due, books it, and offers a next pair only after the test says one is needed.

In production the signals come from data Lenskart already holds (the eye-test record and its date, order history, the face-scan profile, Gold status, service tickets), scored by a model trained on past cohorts and checked against a holdout group.

    Sight Check-in on WhatsApp

    Lenskart holds the date and result of every eye test it runs, but the customer has to remember when the next one is due. Sight Check-in sends one short, plain-language message when a test falls due, with a slot to book. Who receives it depends on the wearer's age.

    WearerRe-test rhythm (illustrative)Check-in goes to
    Under 18Every 6 monthsParent Check-in: the parent who holds the account, with consent taken at the eye test (DPDP Act 2023 needs it for under-18s)
    18 to 39Every 24 months, sooner with symptomsSelf Check-in: the wearer
    40 and aboveEvery 12 monthsSelf Check-in: the wearer. A family member gets a copy only if the wearer opts in

    Guardrails built in

    • No product, price or discount inside a check-in. The message is about the eye test only.
    • At most one check-in when a test falls due and one reminder a week later, then it stops until the next due date. Every message says how to opt out.
    • Nothing is sent while a service ticket is open; the ticket gets fixed first.
    • No fear language and no diagnosis. Symptoms lead to an eye test with an optometrist, never to a product.

    Growth Console

    The screen Lenskart's retention team would open every Monday: whose eye test is due, what each group gets this week, and what that is worth. Uses a made-up group of 5,000 customers so it runs without Lenskart data.

    1Who is due

    Every customer gets a Recall Score from the age of their prescription, their age band, symptoms, screen time and the age of their pair.

    2What we do about it

    Customers are grouped by the action they get. Click any row to see who is in it and how the ₹ is worked out.

    3What it is worth for Lenskart

    A what-if calculator on Lenskart's disclosed India numbers. Change any assumption and the total updates.

    1 · Who is due this week

    Share of the cohort in each tier. Overdue = Recall Score 60+. Due now = 35 to 59, or any reported symptom, or a test at its interval. Not due = the rest.

    2 · What we do about it

    Sorted by expected revenue won back, highest first. Click a column header to re-sort, or a row for detail.

    How to prove it (first 90 days)

    1
    Pick 2 groups

    Children with myopia and adults whose last test is over 24 months old: clear due dates, clear need.

    2
    90/10 holdout

    10% of due customers get today's experience. Measure the gap, not the absolute number.

    3
    Primary metric

    Re-tests completed within 30 days of the due date, then orders within 30 days of the re-test.

    4
    Guardrails

    WhatsApp opt-outs and blocks, NPS, wrong-power tickets, return rate.

    Lenskart brand context schema

    Read from computed styles on 13 Nov 2026 with a headless browser, on Lenskart's India store locator, its Lenskart at Home page and its US storefront, which shares the same token names. From the network this audit ran on, Lenskart's India home and product pages redirected to the US storefront, so the Gold colours are taken from declared CSS variables, not from a rendered Gold page. Lenskart Sans is Lenskart's own typeface, so this prototype uses Plus Jakarta Sans, the face Lenskart renders on Lenskart at Home and on its US storefront. The Lenskart logo is deliberately not reproduced.

    Colour tokens

    Type

    • Lenskart Sans (own typeface) on the store locator and for headings and prices on Lenskart at Home; Plus Jakarta Sans for body text there and across the US storefront. Lenskart Serif appears once, on a store locator sub-heading.
    • Section heading 24 to 28px, 700 to 800, navy. Product title 16px/500. Price 20px/600. Body 13 to 16px.
    • Buttons 16 to 20px, 700, white on navy. Noto Sans Devanagari is added here for Hindi.

    Shape and depth

    • Primary button: navy #000042, 48px tall, 8 to 12px radius, no border. Outline button: 1px #737397 border, 8px radius.
    • Cards 16px radius with a 1px #DBDBEA or #F0F0F6 border; inner panels 12px and 8px.
    • Shadows are soft: 0 1px 16px rgba(0,0,0,.08) on product cards; 0 12px 16px -4px rgba(16,24,40,.08) on store cards.
    • Pages are white with warm off-white panels (#FBF9F7, #F7F2ED).

    Product page anatomy

    1. Photos, 360 view and AR Try On tabs; a "Scan Face" prompt for frame size.
    2. Title 16px, price 20px in navy, with a dashed-border offer line in blue.
    3. One navy "Select Lenses" button, sticky at the bottom on a phone.
    4. A three-step lens flow: power type, lens package, add power. Coatings come bundled in tiered packages, each priced as frame plus lens.

    Voice

    • Plain and practical: "Book Free Appointment", "Not sure about your size? Do face scan for perfect size".
    • Service promises are stated as numbers: "12 Step Eye Checkup", "in just 15 minutes", "Try 150+ frames at home".
    • Offers are loud and repeated: "Buy 1 Get 1 Free with GOLD" on listing cards.
    • This concept keeps the practical tone and drops the offer from every recall message.
