What happens after a Lenskart eye test? A product case study, with a working prototype.

I'm Anchit Tandon, a product manager. I studied Lenskart, the Indian eyewear retailer, from the outside, using only its public filings, its website and apps, and 3,500 customer reviews. This page shows what I found and the one thing I would build. It is my own work, not Lenskart's.

Read the 1-minute summary Try the prototype See the evidence

The findingLenskart ran 2.07 crore (20.7 million) eye tests in India last year. I found nothing that tells those customers when the next one is due.

The ideaThe Vision Recall Engine: follow up people after their first eye test, tell every customer when their next one is due and book it, and suggest lenses or a new pair only after a test says one is needed.

In 60 seconds

The short version

1 · The eye test is the funnel

Lenskart ran 2.07 crore eye tests in India in FY26, up 54%, and about half were someone's first ever. Its own target: 100 million pairs a year needs 80 million tests.

2 · Strong AI, aimed at the first pair

Face scan, 3D try-on, an AI stylist whose users are nearly 3x more likely to buy, remote optometry in 934 stores, and an AI voice assistant that now answers calls.

3 · The pieces of a recall exist, unconnected

Free eye tests, a ₹99 home test, saved prescriptions, lens replacement from ₹199 and one crore members of Gold, Lenskart's paid membership, are live. I found nothing that tells a customer their next test is due; a rival, Titan Eye+, is reported to.

4 · The bet

Follow up first-timers, tell each customer when their next test is due, and sell only what the test supports. My estimate, on first orders alone: about ₹16 cr a year of extra revenue and ₹5 cr ($0.5M) of contribution (profit after product, selling and running costs), in a range from a small loss to ₹10 cr. Most of it comes from the First Prescription Follow-up. Small in year one, cheap to prove, unproven until tested against a holdout.

05 · Shortcomings

Five places repeat revenue may be leaking, on public evidence

In customers' own words

I read 3,500 public reviews of the Lenskart app: the 3,000 most recent on Google Play (28 Aug to 12 Nov 2026) and the 500 most recent on the App Store (29 Apr to 11 Nov 2026), collected 13 Nov 2026. On Google Play 1,946 are 4-5★, mostly short praise. Of the 1,458 rated 1-3★ across both stores, by keyword match 243 mention delivery, 235 customer support, 220 refunds, returns or exchanges and 148 power, lens or product quality (33 of them a wrong or different power); a review can match more than one. Keyword counts are a blunt tool, and written reviews skew negative against a 4.5 rating. Quoted verbatim; names left out.

What this says: the eye test is what customers trust or distrust most, so a recall has to protect the power reading before it sells anything; returning customers notice when a new customer gets a better deal; and nobody in 3,500 reviews mentions being told their next test was due.

ShortcomingEvidenceSo what
1. The second eye test has no owner2.07 crore India tests in FY26, each leaving a dated, saved prescription. No reminder, recall or prescription-age message is public. The CEO's examples of a trigger are collections, such as a wedding range that "ran out of stock very fast".The only trigger a customer gets is fashion; the clinical one is unused.
2. Churn is acknowledged, not measured in public"Consumers do churn." The disclosed "2-year repeat rate ... about 98%" is repeat orders divided by first orders, not the share of customers who return. No retention or churn rate is disclosed.Heavy buyers can hide the people who never come back.
3. First-timers buy slowly, and nobody follows upIndia eye tests +54.3% in FY26 against customer accounts +24.2%. Management says a significant majority of tested people buy and first-timers take longer, and names the fix: "better CRM to bring these people into the fold" (Q1 FY27 call).The gap is deliberate. The open question is who follows up with the slow ones.
4. Gold's clock is the renewal date, not the prescription dateGold "was designed many years back to address" churn. It is "a second pair, lens upgrades, exclusive offers", and management plans to add "eye-care benefits and services on the app". 83 reviews mention Gold or membership; 71 of them are 1★.The fee already covers a free home eye test; nothing I found tells a member when to use it.
5. Trust in the power reading is fragile33 low-rated reviews mention a wrong or different power or lens, and 148 mention power, lens or product quality more broadly. One describes three tests in two weeks with a different power each time.A recall that leads to a wrong pair does more harm than no recall.

Recall is standard practice elsewhere

The UK College of Optometrists publishes recall intervals for its members, two years for most adults. India has no such norm, so Lenskart would be setting it.

A personal call works; an automated one barely does

In one US eye-care trial, a personal call with a letter lifted follow-up from 28% to 38%; an automated call with a letter reached 30% (Callinan et al., 2017). A clinic, not a shop, and a single study.

A rival is reported to do it

Open magazine reports that Titan Eye+'s rewards programme "offers eye-test reminders and eyewear servicing" (6 Nov 2026). I have not verified it first-hand.

07 · The bet

The Vision Recall Engine: six features on top of what Lenskart already runs

No new stores, products or investment. It reads the eye-test record Lenskart already holds, follows up people whose first test found a power, tells every customer when their next test is due, and suggests lenses or a pair only after a test says one is needed. Each link opens that feature in the prototype.

Feature 1Recall Date

When each customer's next eye test is due, from the date of their last test and their age band. A separate priority score only orders the store call list. Both show their reasons.

For: the CRM teamSee the Recall Date
Feature 2Recall Reminder

One short WhatsApp message when a test falls due, to the parent for under-18s and to the wearer for adults. It carries a slot to book and no price or discount.

For: the wearer or the parentOpen the Recall Reminder
Feature 3Recall Booking

The eye test booked in two taps, free at a store. If an overdue customer has not booked by day 8, their store calls once, and only if they agreed to calls.

For: the customer and the storeSee Neha's booking
Feature 4Recall Outcome

What follows the test, set by its result: keep your pair if the power is unchanged, new lenses in your current frame if it changed, a referral if glasses are not the answer.

For: the customerSee the outcome
Feature 5First Prescription Follow-up

Two messages in 30 days to someone whose first eye test found a power and who has not ordered: their saved power and two ways to try frames. Then it stops.

For: first-time customersSee Ravi's follow-up
Feature 6Recall Dashboard

The Monday screen for the CRM team: customers grouped by action, the incremental revenue each group is worth if the uplift holds, and the running cost.

What pays first. On my numbers the First Prescription Follow-up earns the most and the store call in Recall Booking comes second. The Recall Reminder on its own roughly breaks even in year one; it is there because it is what makes the call list possible and because a customer who returns keeps buying. See the sizing

Reuses (already live)

  • The saved prescription and its date, from 2.07 crore India tests a year
  • Store appointment booking and the ₹99 home eye test
  • Free Lens Replacement and the lens packages
  • Lenskart Gold and the face-scan profile
  • The in-house CRM, the AI voice assistant, and Saathi, the store managers' WhatsApp assistant, for the call list

Adds (not found today)

  • A due date for every customer's next eye test, with reasons
  • A reminder that books a test and carries no price
  • A capped, consented store call when an overdue customer has not booked
  • A next step that depends on the re-test result
  • A follow-up for first-time tests with no order
  • A holdout to prove incremental lift

Where the AI is

  • The due date is a rule, on purpose: it has to be explainable to a customer and owned by optometry.
  • A model ranks who is most likely to act, to order the store call list.
  • Language models draft each reminder in the customer's language and answer questions, inside fixed rules: no price, no diagnosis.

What I would not build

  • No discount in any recall message.
  • No AI-read power; the optometrist decides.
  • No message to a child; it goes to the parent.
  • No contact-lens reorder or trade-in in the first release.
  • No call centre; one call, from the customer's own store.

Who owns it

  • CRM owns the engine and the holdout.
  • Optometry owns the intervals and the symptom rules.
  • Store operations owns the day-8 call list.
  • Legal owns consent and the children's flow.

Lenskart's optometry team would set the final ones. India has no legal prescription expiry, so the due date would be Lenskart's own clinical claim.

Read this honestly

  • Lenskart-disclosed: 9.9M India transacting accounts in FY25 and 12.3M in FY26, "over 1 Cr first-ever tests in FY26", ₹1,950 selling price and 64.0% product margin in Q2 FY27, 1.87 units per customer.
  • Why FY25 customers: someone who bought in FY25 reaches a 24-month interval during FY27. The 12% overdue share is a backlog assumption.
  • What a re-test is worth: ₹1,503, from an assumed mix of 40% power unchanged (₹0), 25% lens swap (₹900) and 35% a new pair (one order, ₹3,650).
  • Where the recall uplifts come from: one published trial, in a US eye clinic, where an automated reminder added 2 points to follow-up and a personal call 10 (Callinan et al., 2017). In that trial the call went to everyone; here it goes only to people the message did not move, so 8 more points may be generous. The first-test uplift has no published benchmark and is my assumption. I have no Lenskart baseline; measuring it is the pilot's first job.
  • The swap loss may be light. Lenskart's own lens replacement offer coincided with its India selling price falling from about ₹1,760 to ₹1,609, so I show 6% as well as 3%; at 6% the net falls to ₹1.9 cr.
  • Each person is sized once. Gold members and last year's customers are largely the same people (88 lakh members against 1.23 crore accounts at FY26 end), so there is no separate Gold lever.
  • Not counted: anything after the first order, build cost, and the optometrist slot a re-test takes from a first-timer. Figures are rounded; totals use unrounded values. Every input is editable in the prototype's console.

So the order to build in is the First Prescription Follow-up first, Recall Booking's store call second, and the Recall Reminder third. The return of about 2x is on running costs only; it excludes build cost and the optometrist time a re-test takes from a first-timer.

Why run it if year one is small

It costs about ₹4 cr a year to run. A 90-day pilot gives a first read on two of the three inputs that decide it, what a first-timer's order is worth and how many returners swap instead of buying. Whether the lift is extra or just earlier needs the 180-day read.

What the model leaves out

Only the first order is counted. Customers Lenskart acquired in FY23 bought 3.6 eyeglasses within two years, so a customer who comes back and stays is worth more than one order. I have not put a number on that; the sensitivity table shows the net at a full year of an active account (₹5.3 cr).

Where it grows

The base is the number of eye tests, and Lenskart wants to take those from 2 crore to 8 crore a year. The first-test half also answers the CRM gap management has named itself.

10 · First 90 days

Prove lift in two groups before scaling

Weeks 1-3 · Baseline
  • Pull two past cohorts of tested customers
  • Measure how many return, and when, today
  • Backtest: does prescription age predict the next order?
  • Legal review of consent for existing records
Weeks 4-8 · Pilot
  • First Prescription Follow-up in Tier 2+ stores (smaller cities), 50/50 by customer
  • Recall for adults 18 to 64 past 24 months, and 65 and above past 12; the store call randomised by store
  • Re-tests off-peak, capped at 5% of a store's weekly tests
Weeks 9-13 · Next step
  • Recall Outcome after the test
  • Children's flow, after legal sign-off
  • First read-out at 60 days; the extra-or-earlier question at 180
  • Scale decision; 90/10 holdout at scale
North star

Incremental revenue per due customer against the holdout at 60 days.

Leading

Delivered rate, reminder to booking, show-up rate, re-tests within 30 days of the due date, lens-swap share.

Guardrails

WhatsApp opt-outs and blocks, NPS (Lenskart's customer satisfaction score), wrong-power tickets, return rate, optometrist slots used.

Sample size

To detect a 1 point lift on an assumed 6% 60-day order rate, at 80% power and 5% significance, an arm needs about 9,500 customers; that fits the follow-up and the store call. The recall message alone implies about half a point, which needs about 37,000 in each arm. Both are sized on order rate; the revenue metric and the store-randomised arm need more, set from the baseline variance measured in weeks 1 to 3.

Data it needs

Test date and result, age band, order history, consent flags, ticket status. Events: reminder sent, delivered, booked, attended, result, order.
