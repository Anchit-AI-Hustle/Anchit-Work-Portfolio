Gap analysis

Where Times Health falls short of what users need, and of its rivals

Times Health is not short of good classes. It is short of reasons to keep coming.On the international plan I could see, it costs about three times cult.fit's Indian yearly price, shows the price only after a one-time code, and offers fixed slots with no catch-up mentioned. Across health and fitness apps, only 3 to 8% of users are still active after a month, so keeping users is the hard part. Its two unique strengths, diet coaching for 16+ conditions plus The Yoga Institute's teachers, and its own race series, are not joined to the yoga habit.

I worked on Times Health+ subscriptions at Times Internet, so some of what this page looks at was built while I was there. It reviews the product as a new visitor sees it today, from public sources only.

Times Health cannot out-price cult.fit or out-scale Habuild on general yoga. It can own two jobs no one else does: measurable progress on a condition, and the path from a race bib to a habit. Everything else is a basic to catch up on, quickly and cheaply.
