Gap analysis

Where Times Health falls short of what users need, and of its rivals

Times Health is not short of good classes. It is short of reasons to keep coming.On the international plan I could see, it costs about three times cult.fit's Indian yearly price, shows the price only after you enter a one-time code sent to your phone or email, and offers fixed slots with no catch-up mentioned. Across health and fitness apps, only 3 to 8% of users are still active after a month, so keeping users is the hard part. Its two unique strengths, diet coaching for 16+ conditions plus The Yoga Institute's teachers, and its own race series, are not joined to the yoga habit.

I worked on Times Health+ subscriptions at Times Internet, so some of what this page looks at was built while I was there. It reviews the product as a new visitor sees it today, from public sources only.

In short: Times Health cannot be cheaper than cult.fit or bigger than Habuild in general yoga. It can lead two things no one else does: measurable progress on a condition, and the path from a race bib to a habit. Everything else is a basic to match, quickly and cheaply.

Where users drop off

3. From first visit to loyal member, step by step

Every user goes through five steps: Discover (finding Times Health), Decide (choosing to try it), Start (the first classes), Stick (keeping the habit) and Grow (adding a programme or a race). Each card shows where users drop off at that step, and which ideas fix it.

5 · GrowNo ladder between productsYoga, diet plans and races are sold as separate productsFixed by: Outcome programmes (idea T3), series passport (idea T6), one identity (fix F13)
