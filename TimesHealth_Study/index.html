Times Health+ · yoga and diet plans · Times Internet Half Marathon · problem-first product study · November 2026

Times Health sells a class. People are buying a habit.

The one problem under all the others: Times Health runs three good things, live yoga with The Yoga Institute, condition-specific diet coaching, and a four-city half marathon series, as three separate funnels. None of them is built around the hard part, which is still practising in week three.

The population it serves has the need: 10.1 crore Indians with diabetes, 13.6 crore with prediabetes, and half of adults not active enough. But a visitor cannot see a price before giving a phone number, the classes run at eight fixed times with no catch-up mentioned, people with a condition are sent to a different product, and the race audience goes home on race day with nothing to do next. This study sets out what each user needs, eight problems with evidence, fourteen fixes, and a working prototype of the habit loop.

02 · User needs

What each person is actually trying to do

Five users, what they need, what they get today, and the gap. The problems in section 03 come from these gaps.

The newcomer

"Is this for me, and what does it cost?"

Needs
The price, the trial and the refund terms before giving a phone number; to know the class times fit their day.
Gets today
A long page with no price, then an OTP wall.
The busy member

"I missed today's slot. Now what?"

Needs
A class that fits a changing day, a short catch-up when life intervenes, and a reason to come back after a missed week.
Gets today
Eight fixed slots and an attendance count.
Managing a condition

"Will this help my PCOS, sugar or BP?"

Needs
One programme for their condition, yoga and food together, with a number that shows it is working.
Gets today
General yoga on one page; condition care in a separate product with no public price.
The runner

"Get me to the finish, safely, then what?"

Needs
A plan from registration to race day, mobility and recovery, a fair policy if plans change, and a next goal.
Gets today
A ticket, articles, a medal, and a certificate eight days later.
The business

"Turn reach into renewals."

Needs
One identity across yoga, diet, races and the ET Prime bundle; members who still practise in week four.
Gets today
Three funnels and a race audience that leaves on race day.
03 · The problems

Eight problems, ranked by who they hurt and how much

Each card names who feels it and the evidence, and links to the fixes that solve it.

P1High

No price or terms before you hand over your number

Felt by: every prospect from TOI, ET or paid ads; the growth budget that paid for them.

  • The landing page shows no rupee figure; plans appear only after "Step 1 of 3", a phone or email, and an OTP.
  • A "7-day risk-free trial with money back guarantee" is being tested, but there is no refund policy page; the terms link goes to the generic indiatimes terms.
  • A ₹1 trial "will begin automatically" as the full plan when the seven days end.
See the fixes for P1 →
P2High

Miss the slot and the habit breaks

Felt by: working professionals, parents and shift workers, the people the page itself targets.

  • Eight fixed one-hour batches; props needed (a mat, a belt, two blocks); no recorded or on-demand library mentioned.
  • Progress is attendance only: "track your attendance during the month".
  • The company's own testimonial names the failure: other plans "always stopped working after 10 to 15 days".
See the fixes for P2 →
P3High

General yoga for people with specific conditions

Felt by: people with prediabetes, diabetes, hypertension or PCOS, the largest group with intent to pay.

  • 10.1 crore Indians with diabetes, 13.6 crore with prediabetes, 31.5 crore with hypertension (ICMR-INDIAB 2023); PCOS in about 1 in 10 women.
  • Condition care sits in a separate diet product with no public prices; yoga is bolted on as a free add-on.
  • Pregnancy, gut-health and fat-burning page variants exist in the site's code but cannot be found from the main page.
See the fixes for P3 →
P4High

The first screen is slow and carries 49 hosts' worth of tracking

Felt by: mobile visitors on mid-range phones; cost per acquisition.

  • Lighthouse mobile performance 0/100: first paint 14.7 s, main content 24.4 s, layout shift 1.17.
  • 368 requests to 49 hosts, about 9.9 MB with autoplay video; two uncaught script errors.
  • The tag manager alone is 1.18 MB and blocks the main thread for 5.5 s.
See the fixes for P4 →
P5High

No trust you can check from outside

Felt by: sceptical prospects; marketing, which has to do every sale on owned media.

  • No app on either store; no independent reviews found on Trustpilot, Reddit, MouthShut or complaint sites.
  • Launch coverage is advertorial; the YouTube channel has 10.7K subscribers after about 18 months.
  • Claims such as "50,000+ successful transformations" and "28% reduction in sick days" carry no source.
See the fixes for P5 →
P6Medium

Small errors, in a category that runs on trust

Felt by: careful readers, older users, corporate buyers.

  • "PST" batch times that are not a conversion of the IST ones; an instructor's name spelt two ways; 230K and 221k followers for the same person.
  • Two support emails; WhatsApp support "24x7", "Daily" and "Monday to Saturday" on different pages; copyright 2025.
See the fixes for P6 →
P7High

The half marathon is a one-day peak with no bridge to the habit

Felt by: runners who need training and a next goal; the business, whose event audience does not become members.

  • No link from the race hub or city pages to a Times Health+ trial, a training plan or a post-race programme.
  • The form already asks "First-time runner?", so the people who most need a plan are known at sign-up.
  • Training help is articles ("12 Weeks to Go"), not a plan; certificates arrive eight days after the race.
See the fixes for P7 →
P8Medium

Race-day friction and risk for runners

Felt by: registrants, first-timers and families most of all.

  • "Non-refundable and non-transferable, even in case of rescheduling"; no category changes; tickets locked in one app.
  • Physio and photos only in a Premium Pass at three times the price.
  • The race hub failed to load in 30 s twice in my tests; Delhi in late March is hot, and the race-day air quality was not published.
See the fixes for P8 →
04 · In people's own words

What the evidence sounds like

Times Health+ has no app and I found no independent reviews of it, which is itself problem P5. So the voices here are the closest public ones: the product's own published testimonial, runners and the wider category. Each is quoted verbatim from the linked source.

The absence is the finding. After about 18 months a paid health product with no reviews anywhere outside its own page either has a small paying base or one that lives only in WhatsApp groups. Either way, a prospect has nothing independent to go on.

What works

A credible partner in The Yoga Institute, named teachers, live human classes eight times a day, and a diet product that already covers the conditions people pay for.

What hurts

Trackers from 49 hosts, including several ad pixels and two session recorders, load before the page is usable. On a mid-range phone that is the difference between a trial and a bounce.

What is missing

A price, a refund page, a catch-up option and any path between the three products. These cost little to add and are what the fixes below start with.

07 · The fixes

Fourteen fixes, each tied to a problem

14 fixes shown

F10 to 30 daysSolves P1

Price, trial and class times on the first screen

Show both plans with the per-month price, the trial and the next eight class times before any sign-up. Ask for a phone number only to start the trial.

Measure: landing view to trial start.

F20 to 30 daysSolves P1, P6

One plain refund and trial page

One page for the trial, the money-back promise, auto-renewal and cancelling. A reminder two days before a ₹1 trial becomes a full plan, with a one-tap cancel.

Measure: trial-to-paid, and refund requests per 100 conversions.

F30 to 60 daysSolves P2

Catch-up for every live class

Record each batch and offer the full class, plus a 20-minute "short version" for days with no time. Missing a slot stops being the end of the week.

Measure: share of practice sessions done on catch-up; weekly practice days.

F40 to 60 daysSolves P2

A slot matcher at sign-up

Ask when the person is free on weekdays and weekends, pick their default batch and a backup, and remind them at the right time. Moving a slot is one tap.

Measure: live attendance in weeks one to four.

F51 to 3 monthsSolves P2

A habit loop, not an attendance count

A streak with two grace days a week, a weekly check-in on sleep, energy and the person's own goal, and a rescue nudge after two missed sessions: a short catch-up, not a guilt message.

Measure: members practising three or more days in week four.

F61 to 3 monthsSolves P3

Condition programmes: yoga and food together

12-week tracks for prediabetes, blood pressure, PCOS and back pain: the right classes, the diet team, one public price, and one number tracked each week (fasting sugar, BP, cycle regularity or pain). Medical safety screening before starting.

Measure: programme completion and the share whose number moved.

F70 to 30 daysSolves P4

A performance and tracker budget

Under 1.5 MB on a phone, no autoplay video on mobile, tags loaded after the page is usable, and only the pixels a campaign is actually using. Fix the two script errors.

Measure: Core Web Vitals on real phones; cost per trial.

F81 to 3 monthsSolves P5

Trust you can check

Ask members for public reviews after their fourth week; publish outcome numbers with the method and the sample; replace unsourced claims. An installable app so practice is one tap away.

Measure: public review count and rating; installs.

F90 to 30 daysSolves P6

One source of truth for the facts

Class times, instructor names, support channels and hours, and plan names come from one place and appear the same everywhere. A pre-publish checklist for every page.

Measure: inconsistencies found in a monthly audit, target zero.

F101 to 3 monthsSolves P7

Race to habit: a plan from the day you register

Every registrant gets a free training plan for the weeks to race day, with yoga-for-runners mobility sessions from Times Health+. First-time runners get the gentler plan and a check-in each week.

Measure: registrants who start the plan; trial starts from the race.

F111 to 3 monthsSolves P7, P8

The day after: recovery and a next goal

Results, photos and the certificate on race day; a 30-day recovery and mobility programme; the next city in the series as the next goal, with a returning-runner price.

Measure: finishers active 30 days later; repeat registrations.

F120 to 60 daysSolves P8

A fairer entry policy

A transfer window until two weeks before the race, a move down a category, and deferral to the next city in the series if the race is rescheduled. Physio for every runner at the finish, not only Premium.

Measure: no-show rate; support tickets per 1,000 runners.

F133 to 6 monthsSolves P7, P3

One Times Health identity

One account across yoga, diet, races and the ET Prime bundle's three free months, so a runner can become a member and a member with a condition can join a programme without starting again.

Measure: people active in two or more Times Health products.

F140 to 60 daysSolves P8

Heat and air rules, published in advance

Publish the heat and air-quality thresholds that would move a flag-off earlier or shorten a category, and send runners the forecast and hydration plan two days before.

Measure: medical incidents per 1,000 finishers.

09 · How I would know it works

One number to steer by, and the first 90 days

The one number

Members who practise three or more days in their fourth week. Renewal follows the habit, and the habit is decided in the first month. Trials can grow while week four stays empty; this number cannot.

Early signals

  • Landing view to trial start (F1).
  • Share of practice done on catch-up (F3).
  • Race registrants who start the training plan (F10).
  • Condition-programme members whose number moved (F6).

Guardrails

  • Refund requests per 100 conversions.
  • Live-class attendance does not fall as catch-up grows.
  • No health claim without a published method.

My first 90 days

Days 1 to 30Make it easy to say yes

F1 price and times up front, F2 one refund and trial page, F7 the performance budget, F9 the facts checklist. Baseline the week-four number for the last three monthly cohorts.

Days 31 to 60Make it easy to stay

F3 catch-up for every class and F4 the slot matcher. Turn on the weekly check-in (F5) for half of new members, with the other half as a holdout.

Days 61 to 90Join the products up

F10 for the Bengaluru race on 20 Dec 2026: a training plan for every registrant, and a recovery programme after. Design the first condition track (F6) with the diet team and a medical reviewer.
