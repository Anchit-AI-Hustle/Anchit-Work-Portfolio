Product study · November 2026

Why users stop using Times Health+, and how to fix it

1 · Who the users are
  • First-time visitors from TOI, ET and ads
  • Busy members who paid for live yoga
  • Users with a health condition
  • Runners in the half marathon series
2 · Why they come, and what they want
  • To know the fit and the price, fast
  • To practise most days, around a busy life
  • To improve their condition, with proof
  • To finish safely, then keep going
3 · What they get today
  • No price until a phone number and a code
  • Eight fixed class times, no recording mentioned
  • General yoga; condition plans sold separately
  • A race day, then nothing
4 · What is missing
  • Price, trial and class times upfront
  • Catch-up classes and a weekly routine that brings users back
  • One condition programme with a tracked result
  • A training plan before and a next goal after

Issues likely hurting conversion and retention

The 8 problems, most serious first

  1. P1Pre-signup frictionA visitor must give a phone number or email and enter a one-time code before seeing any price or trial terms.
  2. P2Schedule rigidity: no on-demand fallbackClasses run only at eight fixed times a day and no recording is mentioned. Miss your slot and you miss the day, and missed days are how users quit.
  3. P3Segment mismatch: a general offer for condition-led demandThe users most ready to pay have a specific condition, such as diabetes or PCOS (a common hormonal condition in women), but the main offer is general yoga and their programme is sold separately.
  4. P4Page performance and tracker loadThe landing page is slow on phones and loads code from 49 other sites, many of them trackers.
  5. P5Low social proof: no third-party validationThere is no app and no independent review anywhere, so a doubtful visitor has nothing outside the brand's own page to trust.
  6. P6Post-event drop-off: no event-to-subscription pathRunners get nothing from Times Health before or after race day, so the race audience never becomes members.
  7. P7Content and data inconsistencySmall errors across pages: wrong time zones, one instructor's name spelt two ways, different support hours on different pages.
  8. P8Event-side friction and safety riskStrict no-refund and no-transfer rules, physio only for premium runners, a slow race website, and no published plan for heat or air quality.

Key metrics

Four numbers behind the problems

Pre-signup friction3 steps + a codeA visitor taps, gives a phone number or email, and types a one-time code before seeing any price. cult.fit shows every price upfront.Why it matters: Each step before the price loses users who only wanted to compare, and wastes the ad money that brought them.Source: Times Health+ yoga page · cultpass HOME prices · Screenshots
Schedule rigidity8 fixed slots, 0 recordingsClasses run only at eight set times (5:15 to 9:00 AM and 4:45 to 8:30 PM). No recording or replay is mentioned anywhere.Why it matters: Miss your slot and you lose the day. Lost days break the habit, and a broken habit is how members quit.Source: Times Health+ yoga page
Page load performance0 / 100Google's speed score for the yoga page on a phone. On a typical phone the main content takes 24 seconds to appear.Why it matters: Most visitors leave a page that slow before reading it.Source: My live audit, 1 Nov 2026
Retention benchmark for the category3 to 8%Of 100 users who install a health or fitness app, only 3 to 8 still use it 30 days later (AppsFlyer, Adjust).Why it matters: Keeping users is the hardest part of this business, and it is where the product does least.Source: Fitness app retention benchmarks (Business of Apps)
