Amar Ujala Education · product case study · October 2026

Amar Ujala already owns every piece. Join them up on result day.

Aage Kya (आगे क्या): a new product feature placed directly under every UP Board result marksheet, turning result-day traffic into instant, trusted guidance for families and consented admission revenue from colleges.

Amar Ujala already owns every piece an education platform needs: an olympiad for Classes 1 to 10, a scholarship exam, results pages, Disha Stars counselling and a news app. But each runs as a separate, one-time event that forgets the family afterwards. The fix is Aage Kya: a new product feature that builds one consented student profile carrying a child from the olympiad to result day to admission, with result day as the first revenue moment.

04 · The six questions

Who, how many, why us, and will they pay

1.Who will use it?

Three groups, in order of who decides:

  • Parents in UP's district towns, Hindi-first, on a phone: they pay fees and, for under-18s, must give consent.
  • Students at two decision points: Class 10 result (stream choice) and Class 12 result (course and college). Younger children through the olympiad, via their school.
  • Schools and teachers: they register olympiad students and want class-level reports. Payers: colleges and universities (UP has 8,375 colleges, the most of any state, AISHE 2021-22), buying through Amar Ujala's ad desk.

2.What do they use currently?

Relatives, a teacher, the local coaching centre, print admission ads and admission agents who are often paid by colleges. Online: English-first aggregators (Shiksha, CollegeDekho, Careers360) and Hindi news sites that report admission news but give no personal plan. Inside Amar Ujala: the result page, then nothing; Disha Stars only if the family already knows it exists.

About 43.5 lakh families face a next-step decision every April in UP alone, before CBSE students in UP and the five other states Amar Ujala publishes in. Fake institutes make the cost real: UGC lists 32 fake universities, 4 in UP and 12 in Delhi.

4.Why will this be better?

Amar Ujala already holds the three things competitors pay for:

  1. The moment: families already open the result on Amar Ujala, in Hindi. CollegeDekho spent ₹126 crore (about $13.3M) on advertising in FY25 to find these students.
  2. The trust: a 1948 newspaper with 22 editions in 179 districts (Disha Stars' own figures); it can stand behind rules an aggregator cannot: free fake-university check, payment never buys rank, verification by the product team not the ad desk.
  3. The buyers: colleges already buy print from the ad desk, so no new sales team in year one.

Plus the data no aggregator has: the olympiad history of a child from Class 1, which makes the plan personal rather than generic.

5.Will they pay?

  • Colleges: yes, proven. Education bought 17% of India's print ad space in Jan to Sep 2025, the largest sector (TAM AdEx). Shiksha booked ₹44 crore (about $4.6M) revenue in Q4 FY26 (Jan to Mar 2026) alone, mostly from colleges.
  • Parents: small amounts, proven. They already pay ₹125 to 250 per olympiad. A ₹299 counselling call is a test, not a pillar; the plan itself stays free.
  • Schools: pay indirectly by collecting olympiad fees; a paid school dashboard is a season-two test.
  • Year-one base case, updated to 2026 passers: about ₹1.13 crore (about $119K): ₹38.5 lakh from 15,387 consented enquiries at ₹250, ₹72 lakh from 60 college season bundles at ₹1.2 lakh, ₹2.63 lakh from counselling calls. Every rate after the pass count is an assumption to test; the unknown that matters is the price per enquiry.
  • How the base case is built (The Conversion Funnel):
    • 19,98,317 UP Board Class 12 passers (April 2026 actuals)
    • × 8% open Aage Kya from results page → 1,59,865 visitors
    • × 55% finish the 2-minute plan → 87,926 completed plans
    • × 40% live in districts with active partner colleges → 35,170 students
    • × 25% request a college call with parent OTP consent → 8,793 families
    • × 2.5 colleges selected per student × 70% partner coverage = 15,387 paid consented enquiries
  • Revenue Breakdown (Base Case ₹1.13 Crore):
    1. Consented Enquiries (34%): 15,387 enquiries × ₹250 = ₹38.5 lakh
    2. Admission Season Bundles (64%): 60 colleges × ₹1.2 lakh (print ads + verified profile) = ₹72.0 lakh
    3. Optional 1-to-1 Counselling (2%): 879 calls (1% of plans) × ₹299 = ₹2.63 lakh
  • Year three, if year one proves the rates: the same Class 12 season at the prototype's high settings (15% open it, 35% consent, ₹400 per enquiry, 150 bundles at ₹2 lakh) is about ₹7.70 crore (about $810K). That is before the Class 10 stream choice and the five other states, which are not counted. The low case is about ₹21 lakh, which is why the pilot's stop line sits there.

6.What is the real problem not solved right now?

A Hindi-medium family has no neutral, trusted place that turns a child's results into a safe next decision, with the parent involved. So the biggest spending decision of a child's education is made on agents, ads and guesswork, and the cost of a wrong call is a lost year or fees paid to an unrecognised institute.

The deeper, internal version of the same problem: Amar Ujala sees this child at Class 5, Class 8, the board exam and result day, and remembers nothing between them. Reports expire in 15 days, workshop attendees are never captured, and the result page ends at the marksheet. The gap is not content, it is continuity and consent.

05 · What families face

The family behind the numbers, from published research

These are published surveys, official data and court and police reporting, not my own interviews.

"When I was in Class 12, all we could see were ads of LPU on TV, radio and billboards. When I decided to join a private university, it was the first option that came to my mind."
A former student from Uttar Pradesh on how he chose a university, ThePrint, 29 Sep 2026.

Who they ask

  • Only 9.36% of 4,968 low-income students in Classes 9 to 12, across 25 states, had ever had career guidance. Family (30%) and teachers (13%) were the top influences (UNICEF YuWaah and iDreamCareer, Bharat Career Aspiration Report 2024, via PTI).
  • UP's own counselling pilot put 31 counsellors in front of 14,700 Class 10 and 12 students in five districts (Outlook, Dec 2023).

What a wrong call costs

  • UP filed more fee-refund complaints against universities than any other state: 827 in five academic years, with about ₹4.96 crore refunded (UGC Fee Nivaran Cell, via Zee News, Jan 2025).
  • In Lucknow, a fake admission consultancy lured families "with promises of management-quota seats"; one parent lost ₹45 lakh (PTI, Nov 2025).
  • The Supreme Court took note of a fake medical college in Etah run from "an old, dilapidated structure" in a field, and asked regulators to warn the public to verify institutions first (LiveLaw, Oct 2026).
Show two more findings: drop-off and phones

Where they drop off

  • UDISE data cited for that pilot: nearly 18% drop out between Class 10 and 11, and around 30% between Class 12 and an undergraduate course, the exact moment Aage Kya serves.
  • Nationally, 32.6% of rural 18-year-olds are not enrolled in any education, against 3.9% at 14 (ASER 2023).

The phone is shared, and Hindi comes first

  • In rural Hathras, 89.2% of 14 to 18 year olds have a smartphone at home, but only 26.5% of those who can use one own it, and 11.2% of girls (ASER 2023, Hathras). Varanasi reads 91.5% and 20.4% (ASER 2023, Varanasi).
  • 870 million of India's internet users, 98% of them, have accessed the internet in Indian languages, and UP is among the bottom three states for internet use, at 46% (IAMAI and Kantar, Internet in India 2024).

What this changes in the product: the parent's phone is the channel, Hindi is the default, and a free verification check is worth more to a family than another list of colleges.

06 · How I would run it

How I would know it works, and my first 90 days

One number to steer by, a pilot with a stop line written down before launch, and the two internal questions I would settle first.

The one number

Consented enquiries that colleges confirm became admissions, each season. It is what colleges pay for and what families came for. Clicks and leads can grow while families are served badly; admissions cannot.

Early signals

  • Start: share of passers who open Aage Kya from the result (base case 8%).
  • Finish: share who complete the plan (55%).
  • Consent: share of families with a listed college who ask for a call by OTP (25%).
  • Renewal: share of season-bundle colleges that buy again next season.

Guardrails

  • Payment never changes the order families see, checked every season.
  • Complaints and consent withdrawals per 1,000 enquiries, reviewed weekly in season.
  • The results page shows its main content in 2.5 s on a mid-range phone.

Pilot: UP Board result day 2027, in the districts where colleges sign up first. Continue only if the pilot beats the prototype's low case on all three of 3% opening Aage Kya, 15% consent and ₹150 per enquiry. Below that, stop selling enquiries and keep the free button and the print bundles.

My first 90 days

Days 1 to 30Listen and set the baseline

Sit with the Disha Stars counsellors, the ad desk and the olympiad team. Get last result day's traffic and exits for results.amarujala.com, and where those visits go next.

Days 31 to 60Ship what needs no platform

Upgrades 1 to 3: the "Aage kya?" button into Disha Edge, the results page refresh, and olympiad reports kept in a parent login. Start the consent and parent-verification design with legal, since DPDP Rule 10 applies from 13 May 2027.

Days 61 to 90Price it before building it

Take the per-enquiry offer to 10 colleges through the ad desk and get written intent at a price. Agree the verification and ranking rules with the ad desk in writing. Fix the 2027 pilot districts and the stop line above.

Two tensions I would settle first

Print revenue

Colleges might move money from print into cheaper per-enquiry spend. So enquiries are sold only inside the admission-season bundle, which already includes district-edition print ads, and I would track print renewals of pilot colleges against every other college.

The ad desk and verification

The ad desk is paid on sales; which colleges are verified, and the order families see, must not be. The product team owns the verified list and the ranking rules, written down before launch. The ad desk sells the bundle and the "Paying partner" label, never rank, and the order is audited every season.
