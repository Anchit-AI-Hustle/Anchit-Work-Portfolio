Test the listener and advertiser decisions

Explore whether acquiring a paying listener can cover its cost, then assess what an advertiser received and whether a renewal makes sense.

How long does it take to recover the cost of a new subscriber?

Suppose a city show costs ₹30,000 to promote. Each active paying listener contributes ₹25 per month after variable costs. Choose a result to see how customer acquisition changes the decision. These are illustrative inputs.

150 extra payers

₹30,000 ÷ 150 = ₹200 per payer. Recovering this takes eight paid months. If the typical payer leaves after four months, the campaign falls short by ₹100 per payer.

300 extra payers

₹100 per payer takes four paid months to recover. A listener who leaves after two paid months contributes ₹50. Check the actual renewal distribution before expanding.

600 extra payers

₹50 per payer takes two paid months. Check that the additional payers were caused by the campaign and that discounts did not reduce the ₹25 monthly contribution.

Read the pilot design

Listener product decision

Proposed pilot for one local show. Choose the observed result to see the next product decision. These are hypothetical outcomes.

People open the host’s link but rarely start listening

Check that the link opens the promised episode, the play control works on mobile web and the audio is available in that region. Compare successful plays per eligible visit before buying more traffic.

People listen once but seldom return

Test a visible next episode or a voluntary show reminder. Compare seven-day return among first-time listeners with the current experience, and monitor opt-outs.

Listeners return and a sponsor wants more ad time

Test one placement. Compare net sponsor income per eligible listener, completion and repeat listening against the current ad load. Expand only if the extra income covers production and the loss of future listening.
