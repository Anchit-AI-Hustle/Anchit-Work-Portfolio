How Radio Mirchi makes money

Follow one morning show from the listener’s seat to the advertiser’s desk, then see how Mirchi worked with four real businesses.

One morning in Delhi

Read Neha and the café owner’s story

Imagine Neha driving to work in Delhi. She turns on Radio Mirchi because she likes the presenter and the music. She hears a song, a conversation about the city and a short ad for a café opening near her office. She pays nothing to hear the show.

Now look at that same morning from the café owner’s side. He has tables to fill and a limited advertising budget. He chooses Mirchi because the café needs customers in Delhi. He books a 20-second message to run several mornings that week. Neha is the listener. The café is the paying customer. Mirchi earns money by broadcasting the message they agreed on. This café is an illustrative example.

At the end of the week, the owner has two questions. Did Mirchi play the ad as promised? The station can show him when it aired. Did it bring anyone through the door? A breakfast offer with a code mentioned on air can help him count some of those visits. His answer will influence whether he buys another week.

What changes when the customer wants more than an ad?

Follow the café from a radio ad to a tasting event

Suppose the café wants people to taste its food. Hearing a breakfast ad and trying the breakfast are different experiences. The owner could ask Mirchi to organise a tasting with a local radio host. Now the work includes planning the gathering, arranging the host and telling listeners where to come. The café and Mirchi would agree a fee for that work.

The fee Mirchi earns is revenue. What it spends on the host, travel and production is a cost. What remains after those direct costs helps pay for running the station. This explains why a large event can bring in more money than a radio ad yet leave less after expenses.

At a ticketed event, a second kind of buyer appears. A runner buys admission to take part. A company may pay to have its name and activities at the run. That company is a sponsor. How much of the ticket money Mirchi keeps depends on its agreement with the organiser.

Four real stories: what did the client need Mirchi to do?

A car, a home appliance, a tin of paint and an insurance policy are bought in different ways. That is why these four companies used Mirchi differently. ENIL, the company that operates Radio Mirchi, described the work in its report for April 2024 to March 2025.

MG Hector took the radio host to the showroom.

Think about buying a car. An ad can help you remember the name, but you will probably want to sit in the car and drive it. Mirchi hosts took part in showroom test drives and talked about the car’s features with participants. For the showroom manager, the next question is simple: how many people who tried the car came back to discuss buying one?

Xiaomi took its products to people’s homes.

Instead of waiting for families to visit an electronics store, its Smart Home League brought demonstrations and challenges to ten residential communities. Residents could see the products in use. Posts about the activity were displayed more than a million times, according to ENIL. That tells Xiaomi how widely the posts appeared. To learn what residents bought afterwards, it would also need sales records.

Dulux made the paint shops the centre of the story.

Dulux sells through dealers: shop owners who stock its paint. It ran a display contest involving 20 selected dealers across five cities. Mirchi hosts visited shops, and entries were featured on radio and online. The people Dulux wanted to involve first were the shop owners themselves. A shop could earn recognition for a good display, and Dulux could give its products a more prominent place in the store.

ACKO met people at a run.

Insurance is usually something people think about when they need a policy. ACKO also appeared in neighbourhood activities and a Neon Run across five cities. The run gave people an enjoyable occasion to encounter the brand. A runner attending, remembering ACKO and later buying a policy are three different outcomes. The report describes the event and leaves the sales outcome open.

These are examples of work delivered. The report gives no fee or profit for each client. ENIL FY25 annual report, pages 15 and 16

One number in plain English. For July to September 2026, ENIL said radio generated 53% of its Indian revenue. Imagine all the money its Indian businesses earned in that quarter as ₹100. About ₹53 came from radio. The figure describes revenue before costs. S2

How are ENIL, Mirchi and Gaana connected?

ENIL is the company. Mirchi is its radio and entertainment brand. Gaana is its music streaming service.

ENIL stands for Entertainment Network (India) Limited. It belongs to the wider Times Group and publishes its own financial results. When you quote revenue in the interview, say “ENIL reported…” and give the period. S6 S2

Who pays whom? Imagine a Delhi café buying an ad on a Mirchi morning show. The café pays to reach listeners. A Gaana subscriber pays to listen to music. Both activities sit within ENIL’s business, but they earn money in different ways. S7 S5

Where do Mirchi Plus and Mirchi Live fit? Mirchi Plus is a name readers may see on digital content and app listings. Mirchi Live is where people can find events and campaigns such as Mirchi Freshers. S20 S12

What is difficult to reproduce

Analysis

Local relevance

A useful city personality understands language, humour, neighbourhoods and cultural timing. Treat that relationship as a product asset, with editorial judgment and talent continuity.

Analysis

Advertiser relationships

A local sales relationship can open doors for several formats. The product challenge is making offers easy to understand, deliver and measure without fragmenting ownership.

Analysis

IP and execution

A recognisable format can travel across cities, but execution depends on local demand, venue economics and sponsor fit. A repeatable playbook should preserve local relevance.

How central teams and city teams could work together

  • Central: product platform, analytics definitions, rights standards, billing rules, experimentation and reusable creative tooling.
  • City: programming, local creators, advertiser relationships, event partners and cultural adaptation.
  • Shared governance: deal approval, sponsorship fulfilment, audience permissions and profit after direct costs reporting.
  • Who can make each decision: ask who owns the customer, product Income and cost sheet, pricing, agreed delivery standard and cross-business revenue giving credit for a result.

This is a proposed working model for discovery. It does not claim to describe ENIL’s current reporting lines.

International: a local diaspora business

ENIL’s public profile describes a presence in the USA, UAE, Qatar and Bahrain. Its FY26 operating review discusses diaspora engagement and partnership / licensing opportunities. These are company disclosures, not a fresh station-by-station international audit. S7 S5

Commercial leverQuestion to investigate
Diaspora relevanceWhich language, music and community formats create a repeat habit in each market?
Local advertiser demandWhat customer categories pay for access, and how concentrated are the accounts?
Operating structureWhich markets are owned, operated with partners, franchised or licensed, and who bears the cost?
Rights and distributionWhich content can cross borders, and on which digital or broadcast platforms?
Capital disciplineWhat local profit after direct costs, cash commitment and partner performance justify expansion?

International growth should be evaluated market by market. A recognisable Indian brand can help acquisition, but local demand, rights and execution still determine the economics.
