Business and product terms, explained simply.

A quick reference for the words you may hear in the interview. Every term has a simple meaning and a practical example.

On this pageMoney and financial termsRadio and event termsProduct, growth and measurement termsDates, percentages and Indian number units

Money and financial terms

TermSimple meaningExample / caution
RevenueMoney the company earns before subtracting its costs.Selling ₹10 lakh of tickets is revenue, not ₹10 lakh of profit.
Profit / lossWhat remains after the relevant costs are subtracted.If revenue is ₹10 lakh and all costs are ₹11 lakh, the business loses ₹1 lakh.
ContributionRevenue minus a stated set of costs, often the direct costs of serving that customer or event.Always ask which costs are included. It may exclude salaries shared across businesses, office costs and tax.
MarginProfit or another earnings measure, expressed as a percentage of revenue.₹2 lakh contribution on ₹10 lakh revenue is a 20% contribution margin.
EBITDAEarnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. It leaves out borrowing costs, tax and the accounting charges that spread asset costs across years.Useful for comparing part of operating performance. It is not final profit and does not equal cash generated.
PATProfit after tax: the final profit or loss after all accounting expenses and tax.A business can have positive EBITDA and still report a net loss.
Standalone / domesticResults for the main company on its own. ENIL presentations also describe domestic results.Keep the company’s stated scope with the number. Do not assume all businesses use these labels identically.
ConsolidatedResults that include the main company and its subsidiaries, with the required accounting adjustments.Use consolidated revenue with consolidated profit when calculating a margin.
Cash balanceCash and related holdings reported at a particular date.It is different from revenue or profit. Some of it may be needed for existing commitments.
Working capitalMoney needed while waiting for customers to pay or while paying suppliers before revenue arrives.An event may need a venue deposit before sponsors pay their invoices.
Royalty / content rights costMoney paid for permission to use music or other content.A song available on FM may need different permission for a podcast or social video.
BarterPayment through goods or services rather than cash.A sponsor may provide equipment. That does not produce cash to pay an artist.
Gross / netBefore / after the stated deductions.Net ticket revenue might exclude taxes, platform fees and refunds. State the deductions.
CapexMoney spent to buy or build an asset used over several years.Equipment or some technology investment. It is not all treated as an immediate operating expense.

Radio and event terms

TermSimple meaningExample / caution
FCTFree Commercial Time: the industry label for radio advertising time that is sold to advertisers.“Free” in this term does not mean the advertiser pays nothing.
Non-FCTBusiness outside standard radio ad-time sales, such as certain brand solutions or events.ENIL may present digital separately. Follow the definition in each report.
InventoryThe ad time or other advertising space available to sell.A station has only a certain number of ad seconds in a daypart.
Sell-through / utilisationThe percentage of available ad time that was sold.70% means 70 out of every 100 available seconds were sold.
YieldThe actual price earned for each unit sold.For radio, revenue per ad second after agreed discounts and adjustments.
DaypartA block of the broadcast day.Morning commute and evening commute can have different demand and prices.
IPIntellectual property: a content or event format and the legal rights related to it.A company promoting or executing an event does not necessarily own the event brand.
P&LProfit and loss statement: an income and cost sheet.For an event, list sponsor money, ticket income and every delivery cost.
Break-evenThe point where revenue covers the included costs.Selling fewer tickets than this may produce a loss unless sponsors cover the gap.
Make-goodReplacement advertising or another remedy for a delivery shortfall.A missed spot may need to be aired later under the agreed terms.
Go / no-goA decision to proceed or stop.Do not commit to an event until the agreed funding and operating conditions are met.

Product, growth and measurement terms

TermSimple meaningExample / caution
FunnelThe steps a customer takes towards an outcome.Discover a song → open Gaana → start listening → pay → return.
ActivationThe first useful experience a customer gets.A successful listening session, rather than just an app install.
RetentionPeople coming back and continuing to use the service.Measure the same starting group after a set period.
Renewal / churnBuying the next subscription period / stopping or leaving.A user can still listen during the remaining period after turning off the next renewal.
CohortA group with something in common, such as joining in the same month or through the same partner.Compare Noise users with similar users; do not mix all acquisition channels.
EntitlementThe access a user is allowed to receive under their purchase or plan.Someone who has paid should receive the correct subscription benefits.
CACCustomer acquisition cost: how much is spent to get one new paying customer.Include the relevant campaign, partner and incentive costs.
ARPUAverage revenue per user for a stated period and user definition.₹600 per paid user per year is different from ₹600 per monthly active user.
LTVThe value expected from a customer over the relationship.State whether it means revenue or profit, the time period, and the assumptions. It is not a known fact for a new user.
Unit economicsThe revenue, costs and profit for one customer, order or event unit.Growth is less useful when every additional customer loses money without a credible path to improve.
PaybackHow long it takes to recover the cost of getting a customer.Contribution payback and cash payback are different.
IncrementalityExtra results caused by the change, beyond what would have happened anyway.A campaign may receive credit for a sale that would have happened without it.
Holdout / control groupA comparable group that does not receive the change.Compare it with the group that receives the campaign.
CounterfactualA fair estimate of what would have happened without the change.A control group can help estimate it.
BaselineThe starting result used for comparison.Measure conversion before deciding how much a new version improved it.
GuardrailsLimits or checks that prevent damage while improving a main result.More sales should not come with worse service or an unprofitable discount.
CannibalisationA new offer takes a sale away from an existing offer rather than adding a new sale.A discounted plan may replace a purchase the customer would have made at full price.
KPIKey performance indicator: an important measure of whether the business is improving.Repeat advertiser profit or subscription renewal rate.
GTMGo-to-market: how a business reaches customers, explains its offer and sells it.Choose a customer group, offer, price, channel and sales process.

Dates, percentages and Indian number units

TermMeaningExample
FY26Financial year April 2025 to March 2026.A financial year is not the same as calendar 2026.
FY27 / Q2 FY27April 2026 to March 2027 / July to September 2026.The latest quarter in this study ended 30 September 2026.
H1 FY27First half of FY27: April to September 2026.Six months, not a full-year result.
YoY / QoQCompared with the same period last year / the previous quarter.Q2 FY27 YoY compares with Q2 FY26.
Percentage pointThe direct difference between two percentages.Going from 20% to 25% is +5 percentage points, or 25% relative growth.
Lakh / crore1 lakh = 100,000; 1 crore = 10,000,000.₹10 million = ₹1 crore; ₹1 billion = ₹100 crore.
