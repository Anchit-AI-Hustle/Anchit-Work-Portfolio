Independent interview study · Radio Mirchi / ENIL

Make the trade-offs concrete. Change the assumptions.

Three interactive, illustrative models for an event edition, a paid subscriber cohort and radio inventory. No input is a forecast or an ENIL internal metric.

On this pageEvent edition: contribution and break-evenSubscriber cohort: first-year contributionRadio: sell-through versus price

Event edition: contribution and break-even

All amounts are in rupees, excluding GST. Ticket price is net organiser receipt; no fees or taxes are deducted again. Variable cost is per paid attendee. Sponsorship and ancillary income must be contracted; allocated corporate overhead is excluded.

Formula: contribution = paid attendees × (net ticket receipt − variable cost) + sponsorship + ancillary − fixed costs. Break-even attendees = ceiling(max(0, fixed − sponsorship − ancillary) ÷ ticket contribution), subject to positive ticket contribution and capacity.

Subscriber cohort: first-year contribution

This model starts with paying users. It is not suitable for treating a complimentary hardware bundle as full-price revenue. Annual price below is net recognised service revenue per paid year, excluding taxes. Cost-share and retention inputs are hypothetical, not Gaana disclosures.

Formula: annual unit contribution before acquisition = annual net revenue × (1 − variable cost share) − annual service cost. First-year cohort contribution subtracts acquisition spend. Renewal illustration applies the same unit economics to renewing users, excludes new acquisition and is not lifetime value.

Radio: sell-through versus price

An illustrative period with a fixed pool of available commercial seconds. Changing utilisation and yield does not account for changes in station, daypart, inventory quality, collections or incremental operating costs.

Decision rule

A revenue increase does not establish a profit increase. Use these models to expose assumptions, then add the actual cost, cash and customer constraints before recommending a decision.
