Independent business case · Radio Mirchi

What I would improve across Gaana and Radio Mirchi

Prioritize listening and billing failures across Gaana and Mirchi Plus on iPhone and Android. Then test one regional listening habit. Mirchi advertisers and events are separate businesses.

Station priority · Test advertiser renewal first. Event and publishing investments depend on evidence and partner support.

WHAT SHOULD WE FIX FIRST?

Don't build five features while the basics are broken

Supplied Gaana iOS screens reveal hidden actions and confusing offers; the repeated popup is tester-reported. Public app-store evidence raises additional Mirchi Android/iPhone and Gaana Android validation needs. Start with 28 ranked risks, fix verified blockers first, and only then invest in new habit features.

FIRST · APP REPAIRS

Help people listen without hassle

Stop recurring subscription interruptions, prevent the music player from hiding buttons and show the actual amount and duration of each plan.

Why first? A beautiful new feature won't help if users cannot enjoy a song or trust a payment.

SECOND · SMALL USER TEST

Give people a reason to return

Invite a small group to try an optional Mirchi voice intro or a morning/commute playlist. Compare their voluntary return with listeners using the regular experience.

Why second? Returning by choice is more valuable than opening the app after another push notification.

THIRD · ONLY IF IT HELPS

Try music shared with friends

Test a private Mehfil song room or dedication once music rights and safety are approved. Leave fan clubs and playlist import for later validation.

Why third? Sharing is useful only if friends actually listen and the experience is safe and sustainable.

Who decides? Gaana owns its app and subscription changes; Radio Mirchi contributes hosts, content and regional expertise. These are proposed experiments, not approved releases. Revenue, app quality, user safety and rights must be checked before expansion.

CONTENT & IP: WHAT TO TEST FIRST

Original shows, songs and a mini-OTT slate: priorities

Mirchi audio fiction already exists. Do not treat AI audio stories as a brand-new opportunity. Instead, start from rights-cleared existing IP and adapt only the most promising stories to properly directed, consistent short-form video.

01 · Test first

One proven audio story becomes a mini-series

Test an approved 9:16 video adaptation with subtitles, a recurring cast and unambiguous episode navigation. Proposed target: 18–34 adults, with Unknown age retained. Compare voluntary completion and episode-two return against its audio format.

02 · Comedy franchise

Society No. 420

Original recurring neighbours and family comedy, adapted from an approved audio script when rights permit. Proposed target: 25–44; test character attachment and organic shares.

03 · Original music

Emotional Indian-life songs

Rights-cleared original songs tied to first salary, weddings, nostalgia and heartbreak. Measure saves, intentional replays and music-to-story conversion rather than only impressions.

04 · Online events

Mirchi Live: interactive premieres

Live RJ-hosted, moderated watchstreams where consented audience votes genuinely change a scene reveal or a community setlist. Compete on participation, not generic streaming. See live formats.

Business gate: rights, quality, user safety and working player first. Pilot one format at a time; retain only content that produces meaningful return and contribution after performers, creators, AI, music licences, distribution, acquisition and moderation. Measure local advertiser renewals

Offer a limited catalogue by objective, city and budget, supported by a clear delivery plan and response measurement. Start with a category that repeats frequently enough to evaluate within a practical window. A sales-assisted workflow is a reasonable first version; self-service comes after the offer is repeatable.

  • Primary: extra 90-day advertiser profit after direct costs and repeat booking.
  • Checks that protect service and profit: collection days, deal discount, delivery exceptions, sales time and listener experience.
  • Evidence: matched accounts or a phased rollout; track displaced radio or solution spend.
  • Stop: higher gross billings with lower retained margin or longer credit exposure.

Evaluate repeat events

Put event discovery, booking partners, sponsor fulfilment and the event budget into one operating view. Preserve attendee consent and ticket-platform contractual boundaries. The product should support a faster, better proceed or stop decision, not merely make another listing page.

  • Primary: actual edition profit after direct costs and cash conversion.
  • Customer: entry wait, issue resolution, sponsor fulfilment and subsequent return.
  • Scale: comparable editions meet economics and service gates across cities.
  • Stop: the model depends on uncontracted sponsorship, unrealistic attendance or unsustainable local workload.

Improve content discovery and production

For content continuity, start with one franchise and preserve the destination from social post to story page to app. For AI, observe a real workflow first: transcription, clip selection, tagging, local adaptation or campaign report assembly may be candidates. Compare turnaround and error rate against the current process.

Quality and rights are part of the product

Keep editorial review, talent permission, licensed-use boundaries and factual checks in the release criteria. A faster output is valuable only if it is usable and creates no extra correction or rights burden.
