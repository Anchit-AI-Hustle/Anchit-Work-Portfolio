THE ACTION PLANINDEPENDENT RECOMMENDATION · 23 NOV 2026

Radio Mirchi × Gaana · product case study

What I would do in the first 90 days

First make the music app easy to use. Then give people a reason to return. Only then test what they are willing to pay for.

This is my proposed plan for an interview discussion, not an announced roadmap or an approved project at Mirchi or Gaana.

START HERE · THE BACKGROUND

Two businesses, two different jobs

Radio Mirchi makes shows, works with local advertisers and runs events. Gaana is a music-listening app within the same parent company, ENIL. Mirchi's voices and content could help Gaana build listening habits, but changing Gaana's app requires its own product team's agreement.

♫

Gaana: the listener's experience

Can someone play a song without interruption, enjoy coming back, and clearly understand a paid offer?

◉

Mirchi: the content and relationships

Can familiar hosts, regional stories and real-world communities make that experience more personal?

THE PRIORITY · GAANA MUSIC APP

From a frustrating first visit to a listening habit

The supplied Gaana iPhone screens show paid-plan buttons being covered by the music player, crowded subscription promotions and confusing prices. A frequently reopening subscription popup was also reported. Those are reasons to investigate and fix the experience before spending effort on new AI features.

What happens over the 90 days?

The dates below are an example sequence, not promises. Test and measure four separate experiences: Gaana iOS/Android and Mirchi Plus iOS/Android. The next stage starts only if previous work is safe, usable and supported by evidence. See 28 ranked issues.

  1. 01Days 1–15Understand the problem

    See exactly where people struggle

    Watch new and returning listeners on both Gaana apps try to play music, close offers and choose a plan. Also verify Mirchi Plus app availability, show handoff and playback on Android and iOS. Check actual prices at payment.

    ExampleDoes the ₹709 plan charge immediately? Why is the monthly figure different when we divide it by 12?

    Before moving on: we can reproduce the problems, show screenshots of each issue and explain the real payment terms.

  2. 02Days 16–30Fix the basics

    Let the listener stay in control

    Stop subscription offers appearing again and again, make Close work immediately, move the music player away from purchase buttons and make plan differences readable. Fix current Mirchi Plus login, blank-screen, stream or country-link failures only when reproduced.

    ExampleWhen someone taps a monthly plan, they can read its price and tap Subscribe without the floating player blocking it.

    Before moving on: key buttons work, payment information agrees across screens and people can start listening without unwanted interruption.

  3. 03Days 31–60Test a reason to return

    Bring a familiar Mirchi voice into Gaana

    With permission, try one optional regional-language RJ voice introduction and a useful daily moment, such as the morning commute or chai-time 90s songs. Keep it easy to skip or turn off.

    Example“Your drive home: here's a song from the 90s,” spoken briefly by a familiar, rights-cleared host voice.

    Before moving on: people choose to return more often than listeners without this feature, without making the experience expensive or annoying.

  4. 04Days 61–90Decide what deserves investment

    Test sharing, then make a decision

    Try a small, private group listening room with song dedications, only if playback rights and safety checks allow it. Compare whether people actually listen together and return.

    ExampleShare a song room through WhatsApp, add a short voice dedication and see whether invited friends listen–not just click.

    At day 90: recommend continuing, improving or stopping each idea based on listener experience and the real cost of running it.

HOW WOULD WE KNOW IT WORKED?

Three questions, not a wall of dashboards

▶

Can people play music?

More people start the song they came for, with fewer interruptions and blocked taps.

↻

Do they choose to return?

Count listeners who come back and play music on another day, not just app opens.

₹

Does it make business sense?

Money earned after the extra costs of music rights, voice technology and promotion.

No improvement is assumed. Compare results with a similar group that did not receive the new feature. A paid subscription renewed a year later cannot be proven inside a 90-day test.

A SEPARATE BUSINESS PROBLEM

What about Radio Mirchi's stations and advertisers?

Not every improvement belongs to Gaana. Mirchi also needs better ways to help local advertisers get value from campaigns and decide which events are worth repeating. This is a separate workstream, not four more things for one Gaana team to build.

01

First month: understand a station

Talk to listeners, producers and advertisers in one city. Find the most important repeated problem.

02

Second month: fix one issue

Try one improvement, such as easier campaign reporting or finding a popular show online.

03

Third month: check real results

Did advertisers book again? Did the change save time or make more money after its costs?

Show the detailed station delivery checklist

Days 1–15: confirm ownership and measurement. Days 16–30: agree the problem, cost and an honest comparison. Days 31–60: release one focused improvement with tracking. Days 61–90: review customer results, cash collected and direct costs, then decide whether to repeat it in another city.

One city and one accountable owner come before expansion. Read the business recommendations.

Who would actually do this?

Mirchi and Gaana are different operating teams. This is the proposed responsibility split; it does not assume approval or access to internal systems.

Gaana team

Own the app changes

Player layout, subscription screens, payment terms, experiments and reliable listening.

Mirchi team

Supply the human connection

Host participation, regional stories, local programming and creator relationships.

Finance, legal and data

Check it is safe and worth doing

Voice and music permission, cost, listener privacy and fair measurement.

How would decisions get made?

Each week, review what listeners are struggling with. Every two weeks, check whether the changes worked for real people. At the end of each month, decide what deserves more time and money. A promising idea is not enough if the experience gets worse or its costs exceed its value.

Keep going if people benefitImprove if the signal is mixedStop if harm or cost outweighs value

Three things I would confirm in the interview

  • Which of these businesses would I actually own: station products, Gaana, or both?
  • Which product changes and customer data would I have permission to work with?
  • What real outcome would count as a successful first 90 days for the hiring team?
Show additional planning considerations

Before promising a roadmap, also agree the available engineering and editorial capacity, existing in-flight work, finance definitions, which cities matter most, and who has final authority over pricing and app releases.
