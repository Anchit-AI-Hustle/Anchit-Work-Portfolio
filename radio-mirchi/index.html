Independent business case · Radio Mirchi

Can better campaign reporting improve profitable repeat bookings?

Recommendation: test a one-page campaign report with Delhi auto dealers. Expand only if the station earns more per eligible account after delivery and reporting costs.

Business context and decision

Radio Mirchi sells airtime and advertiser-funded campaigns. In July–September 2026, radio accounted for 53% of ENIL’s domestic revenue. Management reported a 12.6% decline in revenue from selling commercial airtime during the quarter. Improving profitable repeat bookings is one response a city sales team can test. Financial evidence

Customer, problem and evidence

A dealer pays to attract potential buyers to a showroom. The station can document when ads aired and when a host appeared. The dealer holds the test-drive records. The proposed report brings those two records together before the next booking decision.

Documented: Mirchi hosts participated in MG Hector showroom drives. Hypothesis: some dealers would renew if they could judge delivery and response more easily. The published case gives no renewal rate, client fee or evidence that reporting caused lost sales. ENIL FY25 annual report, pp. 15–16

Alternatives and priority

Test advertiser reporting first. A city sales team can trial it with existing bookings and station logs. No new listener app is required. Compare four stations for the same dealer brief.

Event operations become the priority if sponsor shortfalls or venue commitments account for larger losses. Podcast discovery becomes the priority if listeners repeatedly fail to find a show and the publishing team can change distribution. Both require partners and a different test. Review alternatives.

Proposed service and unit economics

  1. Agree on the dealer’s objective, the broadcasts booked and what the dealer can count.
  2. Reconcile the station log with the booking. Flag any missed broadcasts before discussing renewal.
  3. Review aggregate test drives, report limitations and compare the next booking with its delivery cost.

Illustrative renewal: ₹50,000 billed less ₹25,000 delivery and ₹2,000 reporting leaves ₹23,000 toward running the station. With a ₹15,000 discount and ₹12,000 extra host cost, the same renewal loses ₹4,000. Compare both offers in the prototype.

Pilot design and responsibilities

Discovery, weeks 1–2. The city sales lead reviews the last 90 days of eligible dealer bookings. Speak separately with repeat buyers and non-renewers. Record reasons including price, seasonality, delivery failures and unclear results. Change direction if delivery failures dominate.

Trial, weeks 3–6. Allocate eligible dealer accounts to the report or usual follow-up before outcomes are known. Balance prior spend and repeat history. Keep price policy and contact timing consistent. Sales owns follow-up, production checks broadcast logs and finance verifies direct costs. The dealer supplies aggregate counts voluntarily.

Readout. Observe each account for 90 days after its campaign ends. Agree on the minimum worthwhile change and required sample using historical variation before enrolling accounts. If too few accounts qualify, treat the pilot as a usability study.

Success measures, risks and decision

Compare the average money left from repeat bookings per eligible account in each group, counting zero for accounts that do not return. Deduct discounts, direct delivery costs and extra reporting time, including time spent on accounts that never renew. Also track repeat booking rate, days to collect payment and unresolved delivery complaints.

Expand when the improvement clears the agreed financial threshold with adequate precision and service holds up. Revise if buyers value the report but staff effort consumes the benefit. Stop if routine follow-up performs as well or buyers consistently identify another barrier. Track seasonal promotions and account overlap. A test drive alone cannot establish an incremental vehicle sale.

Status: research-backed proposal. The pilot has yet to run. Actual costs, response and renewal effects require station and dealer data.
