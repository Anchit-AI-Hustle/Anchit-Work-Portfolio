The interview answer in one minute

“Mirchi has already experimented with AI presenters, station branding, campaigns, images and event photo delivery. Gaana also reports new voice controls through Apple. I would build on this work with tools that help teams prepare and reuse content, serve advertisers and help listeners find the right music or story. I would start with one workflow, measure the time and money it saves, and expand after the team finds the output useful.”

Existing examples are documented below. Every proposed use is a suggestion for validation. The public record cannot reveal every internal tool, supplier or experiment.

How to judge the money and time saved

  • For staff tools, count the complete task. Include checking and correcting AI output. Saving drafting time helps only when total time per approved result falls.
  • For streaming, compare similar groups receiving the new and existing experience. Track successful plays, return after 7 and 30 days, subscription renewal and profit after content, payment, support and AI costs.
  • For advertising, track approved proposal time, win rate, price achieved, delivery, collections and repeat spend. Include whether a new package takes money away from an existing package.
  • For events, measure demand forecast error, ticket income, refunds, sponsor renewal and actual profit after direct costs. Count vendor commitments made before cash is collected.
  • For a new paid AI product, test whether customers pay and return. Include model usage, audio processing, storage, editing, support and sales costs.

Illustrative calculation. If 20 editors each save 30 minutes a day over 22 working days, that is 220 hours per month. At an assumed ₹500 per hour, the capacity value is ₹1.10 lakh. After an assumed ₹30,000 monthly tool and review cost, the model leaves ₹80,000 in capacity value. These are example inputs. Actual cash savings depend on how the freed time is used.

The first 90 days for AI

PeriodWorkEvidence needed to continue
Days 1-15Map tools already used by each team. Observe real tasks. Record time, errors, cost and who approves the result.One repeated task with a clear owner and enough examples to evaluate.
Days 16-30Build the content assistant or proposal assistant around approved source material. Compare it with a template and the current workflow.Useful output after review, with acceptable cost and fewer total staff minutes.
Days 31-60Run a limited team pilot. Track corrections, rejected outputs and time per completed task.The team keeps using it and the whole task becomes cheaper or faster without a quality decline.
Days 61-90Expand the successful workflow. Test a small listener-facing idea if the data, rights and team are ready.Measured customer benefit, an owner for ongoing support and a clear spending limit.

A transcript-and-clip assistant is my provisional first choice because the team can review its output and measure its value quickly. A large new voice or music product needs stronger proof of demand and rights before investment.

Questions I would ask the Mirchi team

  • Which AI tools already save staff time, and how is that measured?
  • Is Airah still in use, and what did the experiment teach the team?
  • Which Gaana discovery and voice features use AI, and what improved in controlled tests?
  • How much does it cost to produce and repurpose one approved episode or campaign today?
  • What permissions cover talent voices, stories, music, transcripts and new language versions?
  • Who owns the AI budget, output quality and ongoing support?
  • Which customer or business result should the first AI pilot improve?
