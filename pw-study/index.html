01 Overall goal

Identify PW's highest-leverage AI-led growth opportunities, starting with learner needs

I set out to understand PhysicsWallah's business, the learner journey, existing AI capabilities and public customer feedback; rank problems worth solving; and demonstrate how a product leader could move from evidence to a viable growth experiment.

Central question

How can PW improve learning continuity-and earn greater next-stage enrolment through better outcomes?

I selected one testable hypothesis: when a paid learner misses practice, could a manageable catch-up intervention using existing PW tools help them recover? This is an opportunity to investigate, not proof that PW has an internal retention problem.

  • Start with a meaningful learner problem.
  • Reuse PW's existing AI and product assets.
  • Prefer measurable academic value to feature volume.

↗ Acquisition audit · SEO + AEO

How learners discover the right PW course through Search and AI answers

I reviewed a small public sample of PW batch landing pages, the published crawler rules, and Google Search Central's AI search guidance. This is an opportunity audit, not an assertion about PW's rankings, technical defects or conversions.

Observed · Content

Current-year intent is sometimes diluted

The 2027-focused NEET Class 12 and JEE batches pages include 2026 exam/counselling context near the opening.

Improve: lead with the target-year decision, clarify current batch eligibility and update dates, then separate archived exam-cycle news.

Observed · Discovery foundation

Course information and crawl controls exist

PW publishes course tables, target years, schedules and FAQs; its robots.txt links to a sitemap and restricts study/practice paths, which may intentionally protect app experiences.

Improve: audit indexable public landing pages, internal links and canonical/structured-data alignment. Do not remove restrictions without knowing their purpose.

Proposed · SEO

Make the next action obvious by search intent

  • Group pages by target exam year, class, language and online/offline mode.
  • Put reviewed batch comparisons and current entry dates above unrelated updates.
  • Link to the right cohort and explain what's included before login or payment.
Proposed · AEO / AI discovery

Publish precise, source-backed answers

  • Use visible, verified Q&A for real questions: suitability, duration, language, fee and support.
  • Align structured data with visible, current content; cite official exam sources.
  • Keep pages crawlable and useful; Google does not require AEO-only schema or llms.txt.
P0 · Validate

Sample GSC landing pages, queries, impressions, CTR, indexation and qualified enrolment starts.

P1 · Test

Update a matched set of 2027 batch landing pages and compare with unaffected pages.

P2 · Measure

Track non-branded discovery, course-match clicks, enrolment conversion and sourced AI citations where observable.

Limits: I did not have access to PW Search Console, analytics, crawl logs, site-wide schema diagnostics, AI referral reports or paid enrolment attribution. Google doesn't guarantee indexing or inclusion in AI answers. Any ranking or revenue impact remains unmeasured.

03 Evidence-backed findings

Six public observations, each connected to a product decision

These findings come from sampled PW pages, app reviews and published company reports. A review is a customer signal, not evidence of how often a problem occurs. I separate what I observed from what I would test.

Finding 1 · Existing AI products

PW already has a learning-AI foundation

AI Guru, guided practice, Pi Lens, planning and mentorship are publicly described PW capabilities.

Product implication: connect existing tools around a specific missed-practice moment; avoid another generic AI tutor.

Finding 2 · Parent experience

Some parents report missing or inaccurate progress data

Selected Parent App reviews mention attendance, lesson activity and assessment records that do not appear as expected.

Product implication: validate event reliability before launching automated progress reports. See sample reviews.

Finding 3 · Batch expiry

Course transitions can create navigation friction

Sampled learner reviews describe expired-batch access concerns and ask for clearer batch navigation.

Product implication: clarify expiry, remaining access and the educational next step. See the evidence.

Finding 4 · Category mix

Growth is broader than JEE and NEET

Categories beyond JEE-NEET contributed 73.8% of H1 FY27 enrolments, up from 64.8% a year earlier, according to PW's 4 November 2026 shareholder letter.

Product implication: do not apply a single renewal or growth playbook to Foundation, Board/CUET, JEE/NEET and professional exams. Prioritise and measure by category and learner stage.

Finding 5 · Mentorship expectations

Some paid learners question what support is included

A sampled app review says 1:1 mentorship was expected but an additional paid offer appeared after enrolment. One review is not proof of a systemic fulfilment problem.

Product implication: audit plan-level entitlements, show mentor access and response expectations clearly, and test support handoffs before promising new interventions. See review.

Finding 6 · Search discovery

Some exam-year pages mix current course intent with past-cycle updates

PW's Class 12 NEET batches page lists 2027 preparation but opens with August 2026 counselling; the JEE batches page similarly mixes 2027-28 intent and 2026 exam context.

Product implication: align titles, opening answers, course comparisons and update dates to the learner's target exam year. See the SEO/AEO review.

In learners' and parents' own words

I read 1,425 public reviews of the PW app and the PW Parent App, collected 26 Oct 2026. Quoted verbatim; names left out.

How the reviews were read

680 and 677 on Google Play, 68 on the App Store; the PW app ones are from 2026, the Parent App ones from Feb 2024 on. About half the PW app reviews are 5★ praise for the teaching; of the 270 rated 1-3★, 214 are about bugs or support. In the Parent App, 105 of 677 Google Play reviews (15.5%) mention reports, progress, attendance or results, and 61 of those (58%) rate it 1-3★, mostly because the data is missing, wrong or too thin; most of those are from 2024. No interviews yet: ten learner and ten parent calls are in the first three weeks of the plan.

My interpretation, not a population estimate: some reviewers want clearer progress summaries, some describe confusion around batch expiry, and some question mentorship delivery. Self-selected reviews do not establish prevalence or causality. I would validate each with enrolled learners, parents and support tickets before prioritising a fix.

05 Changes I suggest · And why

Start with one learner recovery MVP; evolve only after evidence

The problem to validate. When a paid learner falls behind, knowing the backlog is only useful if they have a manageable way to resume. PW already has Tracker 360, guided practice and daily challenges. The first test uses those tools to see whether a specific catch-up task gets the learner studying again. As a sensitivity check, a scenario with 50% next-batch eligibility and 40% repeat enrolment implies about ₹600 cr a year in illustrative fees associated with eligible non-returners. A +4-point lift would capture about 7% of that scenario. Actual eligibility, prices and repeat enrolment need validation.

First · MVP

One catch-up action

Use an existing practice set and a small in-app trigger. Why: smallest testable change with direct academic benefit; no model or new content required.

Next · If effective

Support and progress clarity

Validate source data, add mentor help where useful, and consider factual summaries where permitted. Why: accuracy, trust and human escalation matter.

Later · Commercial

Next stage and centre trial

Offer relevant progression to eligible audiences with a separate holdout. Why: prove incremental enrolment rather than assuming revenue follows engagement.

07 GTM strategy and expected output

Launch as a learning experiment, then scale only if it earns the right

This GTM plan is proposed, not executed. The first audience is a legally eligible group of paid learners who missed planned practice. Distribution starts inside the existing PW study journey, not through a blanket marketing campaign or new discount.

Phase 0 · Discovery

Validate and instrument

  • Interview learners, mentors, product owners and data teams.
  • Confirm current rescue flows and reliable practice-event definitions.
  • Choose a legal/consented adult cohort unless child-data use is approved.
  • Pre-register treatment, holdout, learning guardrails and economic thresholds.
Phase 1 · In-app pilot

Deliver the first win

  • Trigger: learner misses a verified scheduled practice set.
  • Channel: existing PW app / Tracker 360 and guided practice.
  • Offer: one syllabus-aligned catch-up task; no sales nudge.
  • Experiment: 50/50 existing experience vs. catch-up intervention.
Phase 2 · Conditional scale

Expand on demonstrated value

  • Review 7-day meaningful recovery, 8-14 day continued practice and independent mastery.
  • Add opted-in, lawful progress summaries and mentor support only if beneficial.
  • Test eligible course continuation and centre referrals separately.
  • Roll out by cohort with monitoring and a persistent control group.
Primary outcome

Learning recovery

Incremental 7-day completed meaningful practice vs. randomised control, with repeat practice in days 8-14.

Guardrails

Quality and learner trust

Unassisted mastery, complaints, errors, opt-outs, fairness and cost per extra recovered learner.

Later business outcome

Next-stage enrolment

Incremental paid enrolment and contribution after the eligible buying window, net of refunds and costs.

Scale / stop decision

  • Scale: reliable data, meaningful incremental recovery, sustained practice and no mastery harm.
  • Economics: incremental intervention value exceeds delivery and support costs.
  • Wait: next-stage conversion cannot be claimed before cohort maturity.
  • Stop: no educational benefit, privacy issues, disproportionate harms or unviable unit economics.

Design proposal · not built or trained

What actually needs engineering-and what does not need a new LLM

Separate deterministic student records, predictive ML, content-grounded generation and causal measurement. The clickable demo uses hand-written rules only.

1 · Data quality

Trusted learner events

Reconcile attendance, DPP, tests and Tracker 360 events with server logs; handle delayed sync, offline study and exam breaks. Unknown means unknown-not “high risk.”

2 · Decision policy

Start deterministic

A transparent missed-practice rule can launch the first A/B test. Route only eligible users; child data requires a separate legal basis and approved purpose.

3 · Predictive ML

Add only if valuable

Compare calibrated logistic / tree baselines with simple rules using temporal holdouts, top-k precision, cohort calibration and false-positive rates by segment.

4 · AI intervention

Use PW’s learning stack

Retrieve only authorised batch content; generate a short plan using approved learning assets. Validate syllabus coverage, factual accuracy, safe tone and cost; fall back to reviewed templates.

5 · Experimentation

Prove incremental benefit

Randomise at learner or household level; estimate practice recovery and mastery against existing support. Test intervention uplift and cost per incremental recovery-not AUC alone.

6 · Safe scaling

Observe and abstain

Instrument opt-outs, learning harm, hallucinations, delivery failure and subgroup drift. Pause unsafe flows; keep education separate from behavioural commercial targeting.

Key AI product judgment: a model that predicts dropping out does not prove that any particular message will prevent it. The product earns its place only when the intervention improves learning, fairly and economically.

06 · The first experiment I would run

Before prediction or renewals: test whether a catch-up plan helps learning

Start with one paid online Class 11 JEE batch, subject to age and consent review. Check how the existing experience handles overdue practice before choosing the intervention. If permissions are unavailable, start in an eligible 18+ batch.

Trigger

A learner misses a planned practice set and completes no other set for seven days. Validate the underlying events and suppress the trigger during planned breaks.

Intervention

Randomise 50/50 at the first trigger. Both arms keep existing support and the same messaging cadence. The treatment sees a short in-app plan with a guided question and a link to approved batch material.

Decision

Read the share of assigned learners who complete a syllabus-aligned practice set in seven days. Check repeat practice in days 8-14, unassisted mastery on matched questions, complaints, subgroup effects and cost per incremental meaningful recovery. Read eligible next-cycle enrolment only when that window matures.

At a 40% baseline and a 4 percentage-point minimum detectable lift, a two-sided 5% test with 80% power needs about 4,800 learners across two equal arms. Recalculate on actual cohort volume and account for any household clustering.

See the catch-up prototype · Review the wider rollout design

07 · Conditional rollout

Validate data and learning lift before adding AI complexity

If the first experiment shows durable practice recovery, this design tests the wider programme in grades with a next batch.

Weeks 1-3 · Baseline and go/no-go
  • Week 0: PW legal reviews permissible child-data purposes, verifiable parental consent and marketing separation; without approval, pilot only an 18+ cohort
  • Pull two past cohorts; define drop-off and renewal per category
  • Compare simple rules to a temporally held-out calibrated predictor; evaluate ROC-AUC, precision at mentor capacity, calibration and subgroup errors. Only add ML if it beats simpler rules
  • Ten learner and ten parent interviews
Weeks 4-8 · Pilot the rescue
  • Eligible adult cohort first unless a legally approved education-only design permits Class 10 / 11 involvement
  • 90/10 holdout, randomised by household so siblings share an arm: about 14,500 learners give 80% power that the lift's lower 90% bound clears the 0.62pp break-even if the true lift is +4pp on a 40% base
  • One treatment arm decides scale; if it passes, phase 2 splits report, plan and offer in a factorial to read each part
  • Weekly Progress Report for opted-in parents and learners; read 7-day reactivation weekly
  • AI Tuto arm: half the high-risk group also gets one AI Tuto hour; it scales only if recovery rises 11.3 points, its cost break-even, or renewal rises enough to cover the gap
Weeks 9-13 · Next-step recommendation
  • Results-day recommendations go live for the March batch end; the renewal read follows in April
  • Vidyapeeth trial in 5 centre cities, using empty seats in classes already running, so each trial costs little beyond the counsellor's follow-up, offered to parents of under-18s
  • Read-out of leading signals; the scale decision waits for the renewal read
North star

First experiment: incremental meaningful practice recovery with verified mastery. Later business outcome: eligible next-cycle enrolment versus holdout, assessed after the next-batch buying window.

Leading

Meaningful practice completion, day 8-14 repeat practice, unassisted topic mastery; later upgrade and trial-to-seat rates.

Guardrails

Learning harm or pressure, opt-outs, refunds, complaints, error rates and unequal false positives. Final-year learners can get educational help without a next-batch sales offer.

Keeping the AI honest

  • Numbers are never generated: every figure in a report is filled from Tracker 360 and test data, and the wording comes from a library of reviewed templates per risk tier and language, so the 127M weekly reports carry no per-message AI cost. A report whose numbers fail a check against the source is not sent. The AI writes only the AI Mentor plans, which are costed.
  • Plans stay inside the syllabus: the AI Mentor builds plans only from the learner's own batch lectures, DPPs and practice sets; plan acceptance (the learner starts day 1) is tracked weekly.
  • People read a sample: 200 reports a week in each language are reviewed by a native speaker for accuracy and tone; any error pauses that template.
  • Learning, not just renewal: the first pilot is judged on meaningful completed practice and sustained study, with unassisted concept mastery as a critical guardrail. Increased sales cannot compensate for worse learning.

Read this honestly

  • PW-disclosed: 4.87M online payers, ₹4,104 ACPU, ₹36,625 offline ARPU, 960k premium enrolments. Every uplift is my assumption until a holdout proves it.
  • Double-counting risk: the calculation subtracts an assumed online fee from offline migration but does not prove the learner would otherwise have purchased online.   • The number to watch: the re-enrolment uplift is +4pp on an assumed 40% renewal base. At a 30% or 50% base the same lift is +3pp or +5pp, so this lever is ₹30 cr or ₹50 cr. The weeks 1-3 backtest replaces the base with PW's real rate.
  • The table shows additional collections minus assumed programme running costs-not net revenue, EBITDA, profit or contribution. Where each assumption comes from
  • Re-enrolment +4pp: ET subscriber retention experiments inform my intuition but do not predict a PW uplift. A four-point PW effect is an illustrative sensitivity, not an evidence-based estimate. ET is a news subscription, not a course, which is why the holdout comes first. For range, published re-enrolment nudges run from no effect to +1.5 points with a fee waiver and +14 points at community colleges (see the research table in section 07); my 2-6pp range sits inside that, and the holdout decides.
  • Upgrades are the weakest lever: it rests on an assumed price gap. +1.5pp is about 58,700 learners, 6% on top of PW's 960k premium enrolments; for scale, Vishwas Diwas 2026 sold 35k Infinity Pro plans in three weeks, so treat this as a stretch, not a floor. The ₹3,000 gap is an assumed price difference between a base batch and Infinity Pro, to be replaced with PW's price book; the prototype's illustrative Infinity Pro add-on shows ₹3,999, so ₹3,000 is the cautious choice.
  • Vidyapeeth 1% of learners with a next batch in centre cities is about 8,500 students, 1.8% of FY26 offline enrolments. ₹36,625 is offline revenue per student and ₹4,104 online collections per user; I treat both as a year's fee, an approximation until PW's fee data replaces it.

How the running costs are priced, and who makes the calls

WhatsApp rates are Meta's INR list rates for India, effective 1 Oct 2026, before GST. I price the weekly report as a utility message and offers as marketing. Meta checks each template's category and bills one that mixes an update with a promotion as marketing, one more reason the report never carries an offer; and if it bills the report as marketing (₹0.8631) the report costs ₹10.9 cr and the base net falls to ₹69.7 cr. Mentor cost, AI cost, opt-in, risk shares and recovery rate are my estimates. 253,000 calls of 15 minutes is about 1,220 mentor-hours a week of talk time: about 51 full-time mentors at 60% of their week on calls, and more at exam peaks, drawn from PW's existing alumni mentor pool, or hired new: the ₹3.80 cr of calls covers about ₹7.4 lakh a mentor a year; if capacity runs short, mentor calls are prioritised by educational need, safety and consent-not by potential fees and everyone else stays with the AI Mentor.

Break-even and the downside

  • Collections-only threshold, not financial break-even: one assumed point of re-enrolment yields about ₹10.0 cr in gross collections (4.87M × 50% × ₹4,104). An assumed 0.62pp would match ₹6.15 cr of running cost before incremental servicing, refunds, build costs or foregone alternatives. True contribution break-even requires PW finance inputs.
  • Downside: if all three uplift assumptions fail, the programme creates no incremental collections while incurring up to ₹6.15 cr assumed annual run-rate and ₹3.5 cr estimated build-team cost, before other expenses. Gains from upgrades or migration cannot be assumed to offset a failed renewal hypothesis.
  • If under-18s cannot be scored (see Risks): every lever runs on adults only. PW does not publish an age mix; at an assumed 50% adult share, collections halve to ₹42.6 cr, costs fall to about ₹3.8 cr because parents still get the factual report, and net is about ₹39 cr.
  • Seats: 1% of eligible learners in centre cities moving offline is about 8,500 students: about 61 for each of the 139 Vidyapeeth centres open at the end of FY26, filling seats in centres PW already runs, which PW now says is where offline growth comes from.

The team, and a cheaper rescue

  • Build team, illustrative: one PM, one data scientist, four engineers, a designer, an analyst and part-time language review, estimated at ₹3.5 cr fully loaded annually. This is an additional assumed cost; the model does not establish profitability or positive contribution for the programme.
  • Who owns what: I own the product and the holdout; the renewals team owns the dashboard; the mentor team owns calls; PW legal signs off consent before launch.
  • AI Tuto, a pilot arm outside the base cost: one hour each for the 633,000 high-risk learners would cost about ₹1.08 cr at an assumed ₹17 an hour. Each hour costs ₹17 and each avoided call saves ₹150, so it pays for itself only if recovery rises by 11.3 points (17 ÷ 150), from 60% to about 71%: calls then fall from 253,000 to about 182,000, saving the same ₹1.08 cr and about 14 mentors. So the pilot splits high-risk learners between the plan with and without it, and AI Tuto rolls out only if its arm clears that bar, or lifts renewal by enough to cover the gap.
