What I would build

The Learner Retention Engine: five features on top of what PW already runs

The problem in one line: a student who falls behind in month three has already paid for the year, so nobody notices until they do not come back. At an assumed 40% renewal rate, about ₹600 cr a year of online fees from learners with a next batch never comes back (4.87M × 50% × 60% × ₹4,104); the re-enrolment lever below wins back about 7% of it. PW's AI already wins the first sale; this is built to win the second. It reads the study signals PW already collects, spots drift early, rescues it, and turns results day into the next enrolment. No new content, faculty or capex. Every feature works in the prototype; each link opens it.

Feature 1Drop-off Risk Score

A weekly 0-100 risk score for every paid learner, from lecture pace, tests, inactivity and doubts. Every score shows its reasons; learners see it as an on-track score.

For: the team that owns renewalsSee the risk score
Feature 2Catch-up Plan

Made by the AI Mentor from PW's own AI: guided practice, AI Guru for doubts and Daily Challenges. A human mentor calls on day 8 only if the learner is still high risk. An hour of AI Tuto is tested as a pilot arm.

For: the learnerSee Riya's plan
Feature 3Weekly Progress Report

A plain-language WhatsApp summary each week, sent to the parent for under-18s and to the learner for adults. Parents get a link into the Parent App; learners get one to the AI Mentor.

For: the parent or the learnerOpen the weekly report
Feature 4Next-Step Recommendation

A personal year report and the right next batch, plan upgrade or Vidyapeeth trial class. Not promoted while a learner is high risk; for under-18s every offer goes to the parent, never the child.

For: the learner and parentSee the recommendation
Feature 5Renewals Dashboard

The Monday screen for the team that owns renewals: learners grouped by action, what each group is expected to win back, and the running cost.

For: the team that owns renewalsOpen the dashboard

05 · Shortcomings

Five places the model leaks revenue

In learners' and parents' own words

I read 1,425 public reviews of the PW app and the PW Parent App, collected 26 Oct 2026. Quoted verbatim; names left out.

How the reviews were read

680 and 677 on Google Play, 68 on the App Store; the PW app ones are from 2026, the Parent App ones from Feb 2024 on. About half the PW app reviews are 5★ praise for the teaching; of the 270 rated 1-3★, 214 are about bugs or support. In the Parent App, 105 of 677 Google Play reviews (15.5%) mention reports, progress, attendance or results, and 61 of those (58%) rate it 1-3★, mostly because the data is missing, wrong or too thin; most of those are from 2024. No interviews yet: ten learner and ten parent calls are in the first three weeks of the plan.

What this says: many parents want a pushed, periodic summary and do not trust the one they have to go and find; the end of a batch currently feels like a loss, not a next step; and mentorship promises have to be ones PW can keep, which is why human calls here are capped.

ShortcomingEvidenceSo what
1. Repeat business is not visibleNo renewal or repeat-purchase rate is disclosed in the FY26 letter, on the Q1 FY27 call or in the Q2 FY27 results; retention is only mentioned in passing. Fees are paid upfront, so drift in month three still counts as revenue.Drop-off hides until next year's non-renewal.
2. Retention tools are scattered and pull-basedTracker 360 in some plans, Prep Meter in Infinity, 1:1 mentorship in Infinity Pro, parent data in a separate app that has to be opened.The base-plan learners who drift most get the least help.
3. AI targets the first saleLead routing +10% revenue per lead; voice bots −75% counselling cost. No disclosed AI aimed at retention or renewal.AI wins the first sale; nothing aims at the second.
4. Revenue is calendar-bound79% of FY26 collections came March to September; Vishwas Diwas took ₹205 cr in 3 weeks; a NEET shift moved 1-1.5 lakh enrolments out of Q1.Peaks are mass events; next steps are not yet personal.
5. Offline growth must come from existing centresOffline ARPU −9% on mix; expansion now "opportunistic"; margin rests on ARPU and seat utilisation (1.77 → 1.99).PW's best offline leads are its own online learners.

FindingSourceWhat it changed here

Read this honestly

  • PW-disclosed: 4.87M online payers, ₹4,104 ACPU, ₹36,625 offline ARPU, 960k premium enrolments. Every uplift is my assumption until a holdout proves it.
  • No double counting: a learner who moves to Vidyapeeth counts only for the fee above the online fee they would have paid, and an upgrade is a separate in-year payment. Final-year learners get no next-batch offer.
  • The number to watch: the re-enrolment uplift is +4pp on an assumed 40% renewal base. At a 30% or 50% base the same lift is +3pp or +5pp, so this lever is ₹30 cr or ₹50 cr. The weeks 1-3 backtest replaces the base with PW's real rate.
  • Collections, recognised over the batch, net only of the programme's running costs; the team that builds it is costed below, and serving the extra learners is not included. Renewals need no lead or counselling spend, so their margin should sit above PW's 14.1% EBITDA average.
Where each assumption comes from
  • Re-enrolment +4pp: my onboarding experiments at ET lifted subscriber retention 10%; on an assumed 40% renewal base that is 4pp. ET is a news subscription, not a course, which is why the backtest comes first. For range, published re-enrolment nudges run from no effect to +1.5 points with a fee waiver and +14 points at community colleges (see the research table in section 07); my 2-6pp range sits inside that, and the holdout decides.
  • Upgrades +1.5pp is about 58,700 learners, 6% on top of PW's 960k premium enrolments; for scale, Vishwas Diwas 2026 sold 35k Infinity Pro plans in three weeks, so treat this as a stretch, not a floor. The ₹3,000 gap is an assumed price difference between a base batch and Infinity Pro, to be replaced with PW's price book; the prototype's illustrative Infinity Pro add-on shows ₹3,999, so ₹3,000 is the cautious choice.
  • Vidyapeeth 1% of learners with a next batch in centre cities is about 8,500 students, 1.8% of FY26 offline enrolments. ₹36,625 is offline revenue per student and ₹4,104 online collections per user; I treat both as a year's fee, an approximation until PW's fee data replaces it.
  • 50% have a next batch (it leaves out final-year school learners and one-shot exams once cleared), 35% live in centre cities, and the 960k premium enrolments (Curious Jr Power Batch, Infinity, Infinity Pro) are treated as learners already on premium. Figures are rounded; totals use unrounded values. Every input is editable in the prototype's Renewals Dashboard.

How the running costs are priced, and who makes the calls

WhatsApp rates are Meta's INR list rates for India, effective 1 Oct 2026, before GST. I price the weekly report as a utility message and offers as marketing. Meta checks each template's category and bills one that mixes an update with a promotion as marketing, one more reason the report never carries an offer; and if it bills the report as marketing (₹0.8631) the report costs ₹10.9 cr and the base net falls to ₹69.7 cr. Mentor cost, AI cost, opt-in, risk shares and recovery rate are my estimates. 253,000 calls of 15 minutes is about 1,220 mentor-hours a week of talk time: about 51 full-time mentors at 60% of their week on calls, and more at exam peaks, drawn from PW's existing alumni mentor pool; if capacity runs short, calls go first to the learners with the most fees at stake and everyone else stays with the AI Mentor.

Break-even and the downside

  • Break-even: each 1pp of re-enrolment is worth ₹10.0 cr (4.87M × 50% × ₹4,104), so a lift of 0.62pp pays the ₹6.15 cr running cost on its own.
  • If re-enrolment does not move at all: the Vidyapeeth and upgrade levers still net about ₹39.2 cr (₹27.7 + ₹17.6 − ₹6.15 cr).
  • If under-18s cannot be scored (see Risks): every lever runs on adults only. PW does not publish an age mix; at an assumed 50% adult share, collections halve to ₹42.6 cr, costs fall to about ₹3.8 cr because parents still get the factual report, and net is about ₹39 cr.
  • Seats: 1% of eligible learners in centre cities moving offline is about 8,500 students, about 23 per centre across 370 centres, filling seats in centres PW already runs, which PW now says is where offline growth comes from.

The team, and a cheaper rescue

  • Build team, my estimate: one PM, one data scientist, four engineers, a designer and an analyst, about ₹3.5 cr a year fully loaded at Indian market pay. It reuses PW's data and AI platforms, so year-one net after the team is still about ₹76 cr at base.
  • Who owns what: I own the product and the holdout; the renewals team owns the dashboard; the mentor team owns calls; PW legal signs off consent before launch.
  • AI Tuto, a pilot arm outside the base cost: one hour each for the 633,000 high-risk learners would cost about ₹1.1 cr at ₹17 an hour. Each hour costs ₹17 and each avoided call saves ₹150, so it pays for itself only if recovery rises by 11.3 points (17 ÷ 150), from 60% to about 71%: calls then fall from 253,000 to about 182,000, saving ₹1.07 cr and about 14 mentors. So the pilot splits high-risk learners between the plan with and without it, and AI Tuto rolls out only if its arm clears that bar, or lifts renewal by enough to cover the gap.
10 · First 90 days

Prove it can predict, then prove lift, before scaling

The pilot uses grades that have a next batch to sell, so renewal can actually be measured.

Weeks 1-3 · Baseline and go/no-go
  • Week 0: PW legal confirms the children's exemption covers the score; if not, the pilot runs on 18+ categories instead
  • Pull two past cohorts; define drop-off and renewal per category
  • Backtest: week-6 risk must rank next-year non-renewal at an AUC of 0.70 or better. If not, ship a results-day tool only
  • Ten learner and ten parent interviews
Weeks 4-8 · Pilot the rescue
  • Class 10 and Class 11 (JEE, NEET): both have a next batch
  • 90/10 holdout: about 13,300 learners detect +4pp on a 40% base (80% power, 5% significance), randomised by household so siblings share an arm
  • Arms inside the 90%: report only, report + plan, full engine, so each part's lift can be read
  • Weekly Progress Report for opted-in parents and learners; read 7-day reactivation weekly
  • AI Tuto arm: half the high-risk group also gets one AI Tuto hour; it scales only if recovery rises 11.3 points, its cost break-even, or renewal rises enough to cover the gap
Weeks 9-13 · Next-step recommendation
  • Timed to a batch-end window, so the read-out includes real renewal decisions
  • Vidyapeeth trial in 5 centre cities, offered to parents of under-18s
  • Read-out of leading signals; the scale decision waits for the renewal read
North star

Next-cycle enrolment within 30 days of batch end or results, read against the holdout.

Leading

7-day reactivation, lecture pace, upgrade rate, trial-to-seat rate.

Guardrails

Opt-outs, refund rate, app rating, parent complaints. Droppers join later as a rescue-only group: they have no next batch.

Renewal can only be read when a batch ends. For a pilot starting in December 2026, Class 10 and 11 batches end around March 2027, so the first renewal read is around April 2027; weeks 4-8 read leading signals in the meantime.

Keeping the AI honest

  • Numbers are never generated: every figure in a report is filled from Tracker 360 and test data by template; the AI writes only the wording around it, and a report whose numbers fail a check against the source is not sent.
  • Plans stay inside the syllabus: the AI Mentor builds plans only from the learner's own batch lectures, DPPs and practice sets; plan acceptance (the learner starts day 1) is tracked weekly.
  • People read a sample: 200 reports a week in each language are reviewed by a native speaker for accuracy and tone; any error pauses that template.
  • Learning, not just renewal: test-score change against the holdout is a secondary metric, so the engine cannot win renewals while learning gets worse.
