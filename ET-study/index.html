Why ET Prime members are unhappy, and how to fix it

What it is
  • The Economic Times: business news, on the web and in an app.
  • ET Markets: the stock market app and section.
  • ET Prime: the paid membership, ₹2,699 for 12 months on the web.
What goes wrong
  • Paying members still get ads, pop-ups and sales calls.
  • Some are locked out, charged twice or cannot get an invoice.
  • Prices and offers contradict each other.
  • Pages are slow on phones, and Google sends fewer readers each year.
What I would do
  • Never show ads, pop-ups or sales calls to someone who has paid.
  • Self-serve access, invoices and cancelling, in one or two taps.
  • One price list, one offer at a time.
  • A "My Stocks" front page built around what you own.

The fix is not another offer. It is a member-first ET: one entitlement, one price book, no ads or sales calls for people who have paid, and a reason to open ET every day that does not depend on Google. Fixes F1 to F4 cost little and can ship inside 90 days.

02 · User needs

What each person comes to ET to do

Four users, what they need, what they get today, and the gap. The problems in section 03 come from these gaps.

The reader

"Tell me what matters, fast."

Needs
The story on a phone in seconds, a notification that opens that story, and a short daily brief.
Gets today
A page that keeps loading for 25 s and 1,364 requests, with a blank ad slot above the headline, and notifications that open the home page.
The investor

"What does this mean for my stocks?"

Needs
News and analysis about what they own, prices they can trust, and tools that keep their data.
Gets today
A general markets feed; the portfolio tracker removed without an export.
The member

"I paid. Let me read in peace."

Needs
Access everywhere they sign in, few ads, no sales calls, an invoice and a cancel button they can find.
Gets today
Pop-ups for the plan they own, paywalls, calls, and an email to request an invoice.
The business

"Renewals, and reach we own."

Needs
Members who renew because they use ET every week, and a daily audience that does not depend on Google.
Gets today
A parent company, Times Internet, earning 65 to 70% of revenue from advertising, and a funnel built around the first sale.
03 · The problems

Eight problems, ranked by who they hurt and how much

Critical means revenue or legal exposure now. Each card names who feels it, the evidence, and links to the fixes that solve it.

P1Critical

Paying members are treated like prospects

Felt by: ET Prime members, most of all multi-year buyers; then renewals.

  • Members report full-screen and video ads, and launch pop-ups for the plan they already bought, while the paywall promises "90% less ads".
  • In 133 recent negative Google Play reviews of the ET app, 16 are about ads and 10 about being asked to subscribe when already subscribed.
  • 28 complaints about sales calls across the four app samples in 12 months, some after cancelling.
See the fixes for P1 →
P2Critical

Paid, but locked out, double-charged or unable to get an invoice

Felt by: new and renewing members, the care team; regulators watch this.

  • Members locked out of Prime stories, AI audio or ePaper; one paid ₹2,599 for a year and was charged ₹2,599 again a month later.
  • Invoices only by emailing the care desk; reviewers waiting 21 to 30+ days. No refund policy.
  • Users say cancelling auto-pay means emailing; the FAQ says it is in My Subscriptions. RBI's 2026 e-mandate rules let a customer withdraw a mandate any time.
  • 129 of 133 recent negative ET app reviews got the same templated reply.
See the fixes for P2 →
P3High

Too many offers, and they contradict each other

Felt by: first-time buyers comparing offers; the business, through discount leakage.

  • Nine distinct upsell surfaces on mobile web; one Prime paywall holds 12 buttons.
  • A "Get Free Trial" badge on the home page, while the FAQ says there is no trial.
  • TOI+ sells the same ETPrime stories and tools at the same ₹2,699; the HTML of the old plans page still carries coupon terms that expired in 2023.
See the fixes for P3 →
P4High

Half the audience and a fraction of the subscribers of Moneycontrol

Felt by: the business, in both ad and subscription revenue.

  • About 1.75 lakh ET Prime subscribers (Crisil, June 2025) against the 10 lakh+ Moneycontrol Pro announced in October 2024: different sources and dates, but a wide gap. ET's own "10 Lakh+ members" claim is not reconciled with Crisil's count.
  • Moneycontrol Pro starts at ₹499 a year in-app; ET Prime is ₹2,399 to ₹3,199 a year in-app.
  • Advertising is 65 to 70% of Times Internet's revenue, and Crisil flags "continued weak operating profitability".
See the fixes for P4 →
P5High

The mobile web is heavy and ad-dense

Felt by: every mobile reader; advertisers, through viewability; search ranking.

  • Home: 1,364 requests from 285 hosts, load event at 25.5 s, 14 ad frames.
  • A blank reserved ad slot fills about a third of the first phone screen above the headline.
  • Lighthouse mobile: Markets 29/100, Prime article 35 with layout shift 0.225, the checkout 34 with its main content at 19.6 s.
See the fixes for P5 →
P6High

The top of the funnel depends on Google, and Google is sending less

Felt by: the business, the newsroom, every future subscriber.

  • Google Search referrals to publishers fell 40.2% in a year; AI chatbots send 0.01% of traffic.
  • Semrush estimates ET's visits fell from 64.6M in July to 49.2M in September 2026.
  • Zerodha Pulse, a free aggregator from the broker, shows ET headlines alongside other publishers'.
See the fixes for P6 →
P7High

ET Markets lost a tool investors relied on, and its data trust

Felt by: active investors, members who bought ET Prime for the tools.

  • The ET Portfolio tracker went around June 2026 with no notice and no export; 19 of 79 recent negative ET Markets reviews mention it.
  • Complaints of prices that are not real-time and old "buy" calls still shown.
  • Seven crash or not-opening reviews of ET Markets on iOS between 8 and 11 Oct 2026.

P8Medium

Felt by: daily readers; retention.

  • Tapping a notification opens the home page and the story is gone (several reports, Nov 2025 to Jul 2026).
  • Notification and email volume that ignores unsubscribes; forced weekly updates.
  • The home page's "Top Prime Articles" widget still links 2023 stories.

What works

The Prime paywall is inline, not a pop-over: the synopsis, an AI conversation-style audio version and the opening paragraph are readable first. No interstitial appeared in the first 15 seconds.

What hurts

About a third of the first screen is an empty ad slot above the headline on the home page and the Prime article. Google's Core Web Vitals ask for main content within 2.5 s and layout shift of 0.1 or less.

The checkout

The page where a reader decides to pay moves 17.8 MB before it finishes loading, more than a Prime article and its ads combined. Every second there costs conversions the sales team then has to buy back by phone.

06 · Root causes

Why the same problems keep coming back

Eight problems, three causes, as they look from the outside. Each cause sits between teams, which is why fixing symptoms one at a time rarely sticks.

No one visibly owns "the member"

From the outside, it looks as if ads, subscription, sales, the apps and TOI+ each optimise their own number. A member therefore shows up as ad inventory to one system, a lead to another and an expired entitlement to a third. P1, P2, P3.

The first sale is visible, the second is not

Bonus-month bundles, callbacks and Diwali sales all aim at acquisition. Renewal, the thing that pays for it, has no visible owner: no value receipt, no self-serve invoice, a templated reply to every complaint. P1, P2, P4.

Reach is rented, not owned

Search brings readers in and ad-heavy pages monetise them once. With search falling, ET needs a daily reason to come back that it controls: a watchlist, a briefing, a notification that works. P5, P6, P7, P8.

ET wins on

  • Newsroom depth and Prime long-form; the ET print edition and ePaper.
  • The widest bundle: HBR, TOI, Docubay, stock reports, SEBI-registered analysts.
  • A large app base: 1.35 crore ET and 53.8 lakh ET Markets installs on Google Play.

ET loses on

  • Price at the entry point, and a monthly plan on the web.
  • Reach and time spent (Comscore).
  • Page weight, and trust once a reader has paid.

ET does not need to be the cheapest. It needs the member to feel the difference between ₹499 elsewhere and ₹2,699 here, every week. Today a paying member sees ads, pop-ups and sales calls, which is exactly the experience they paid to leave.

08 · The fixes

Fourteen fixes, each tied to a problem

Each fix names the problems it solves and how long it would take; the 90-day plan below sets the order. Filter by who each one serves, or use the links on the problem cards.

14 fixes shown

F10 to 30 daysSolves P1

A member is never sold to

Every logged-in member is suppressed from upsell pop-ups, "Subscribe" buttons, sale banners and the sales dialer list. One flag, read by web, apps and the calling system.

Why it is cheap: about 1.75 lakh members against 3.14 crore monthly visitors is 0.6% of the audience; even reading five times as much, under 3% of page views. On ET's own "10 lakh+" claim it is about 3% of visitors and roughly 16% of page views, still a minority.

F20 to 60 daysSolves P1

Keep the "90% less ads" promise, and measure it

One ad layout for members: no pop-up, interstitial or video units, at most one native slot per article. Publish the rule in the FAQ and track ad impressions served to members as a number that should fall to the promise.

Measure: ad impressions per member page view, against non-members.

F30 to 60 daysSolves P2, P1

"I paid, let me in" in one tap

One entitlement record across the ET web, both apps, iOS, Android and TOI+. A "Restore my membership" button re-checks the payment and unlocks on the spot. Any paywall shown to an active member raises an alert.

Measure: paywalls shown to active members, target zero.

F40 to 60 daysSolves P2

Self-serve billing that meets the e-mandate rules

Download the GST invoice in My Subscriptions; cancel auto-renew in two taps in every app; a reminder seven days before renewal as well as the 24-hour pre-debit notice; automatic refund of a duplicate debit within 48 hours.

Measure: billing tickets per 1,000 members; days to invoice, target zero.

F50 to 30 daysSolves P3

One price book, one offer at a time

Web, apps and TOI+ read one price list. Remove the "Get Free Trial" badge or launch a real trial; give each paywall one primary and one secondary button, not twelve; delete the expired 2023 coupon terms still served in the page.

Measure: paywall view to checkout start.

F61 to 3 monthsSolves P3, P4

Make ET Prime and TOI+ different products

Today ₹2,699 buys either, and both list ETPrime stories and the market tools. Position TOI+ as the general-news membership and ET Prime as the investor's; offer the two together as a clearly priced bundle.

Measure: cannibalisation between the two storefronts.

F71 to 3 monthsSolves P4

A clear entry tier on the web

A monthly plan and a lower-priced markets tier on the web, as Mint and Moneycontrol have, with the full Prime as the upgrade. Built on F5's single price book, so it adds a step, not more offers, and tested against today's 6+6 bundle on randomly assigned new visitors before rolling out.

Measure: new payers, and the share who upgrade within six months.

F81 to 3 monthsSolves P1, P4

Assisted sales by consent, not by volume

Call only people who asked for a callback or showed real intent (several paywall hits, a started checkout). Never a member, never after "no", one number, set hours. AI call scoring checks every call against those rules.

Measure: revenue per call; complaints per 1,000 calls.

F90 to 60 daysSolves P7

Give investors their portfolio back

Let anyone who used ET Portfolio download their holdings as a file, and import them into Portfolio X-ray or a watchlist. For the future, a written rule: 60 days' notice and an export before any tool is removed.

Measure: share of former users who re-import.

F101 to 3 monthsSolves P7, P8

Release quality gates

Staged rollouts with a crash-free gate (99.5% of sessions) before 100%; market data labelled with its time and source on every quote; old analyst calls shown with their date and outcome, or removed.

Measure: crash-free sessions per release.

F110 to 30 daysSolves P8, P6

Notifications that open the story

Fix deep links so a tap lands on the article; cap volume per day by topic; honour unsubscribes everywhere; let readers pick topics and stocks. Refresh or retire the "Top Prime Articles" widget that shows 2023 stories.

Measure: notification open to article read rate.

F121 to 3 monthsSolves P5

A performance budget for every page

No reserved ad slot above the headline on phones; ad units below the first screen load as you scroll; a cap on requests and third-party hosts per page; the checkout under 2 MB.

Measure: Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile on real phones, and ad revenue per session.

F133 to 9 monthsSolves P6, P4, P7

My Stocks: ET around what you own

A watchlist or imported portfolio becomes the front page: every ET story, result and analyst note about your stocks, with a two-line "what it means for you". A morning and closing brief on the app or WhatsApp. This is the daily reason to open ET that Google cannot take away, and the clearest reason to pay ₹2,699 over ₹499.

Measure: members active three or more days a week.

F143 to 9 monthsSolves P6, P4

A value receipt and an answers layer

A monthly note to each member: stories read, tools used, ads avoided, and what is coming. Plus "Ask ET", answers drawn only from ET's own reporting with links to the stories, so readers who now ask AI chatbots get ET's answer and a reason to sign in.

Measure: renewal rate of members who open the receipt; signed-in share of answer users.

10 · How I would know it works

One number to steer by, and the first 90 days

The one number

Members who use ET three or more days a week. Renewal follows habit. New subscriptions can rise while members drift; weekly engaged members cannot rise while members are badly served.

Early signals

  • Paywalls shown to active members: zero.
  • Billing and access tickets per 1,000 members.
  • Notification tap to article read.
  • Members with a watchlist or portfolio set up.

Guardrails

  • Ad revenue per non-member session holds.
  • Sales complaints per 1,000 calls fall.
  • Core Web Vitals pass on the top 20 templates.

What one point of renewal is worth

An illustration on public numbers, not a forecast: if all of about 1.75 lakh members came up for renewal, one point would be 1,750 renewals; at the ₹2,699 web price, which includes GST, that is about ₹47 lakh a year, before any upgrade. Multi-year plans renew less often, and the price after GST, bundles and discounts is lower, so read it as an upper bound; the direction holds.

My first 90 days

Days 1 to 30Stop the bleeding

Ship F1 (no selling to members), F5 (one offer at a time) and F11 (notification deep links). Put a number on paywalls shown to active members and on billing tickets, and report both weekly.

Days 31 to 60Make paying painless

F2 (the member ad layout), F3 (restore my membership), F4 (self-serve invoices and cancellation) and F9 (portfolio export). Agree the consent rules for assisted sales (F8) with the sales lead, in writing.

Days 61 to 90Test the bigger bets

A randomised test of a web entry tier (F7) against today's bundle, and a closed beta of My Stocks (F13) with 5,000 members, with a holdout group to prove the lift in weekly use.

11 · What could go wrong

The trade-offs, said out loud

Ad revenue from members

Fewer ads for members costs some inventory. On the public numbers members are under 3% of page views, and a renewal is worth more than the ads they would have seen. Measure it in the first month and decide on data.

Fewer sales calls

Consent-based calling will lower volume. Revenue per call should rise, because every call goes to someone who asked. If revenue falls below the old line for two months, widen the intent rules, not the call list.

A cheaper entry tier

It can pull full-price buyers down. That is why F7 starts as a randomised test with an upgrade path, and is judged on revenue per new payer after six months, not on sign-ups.
