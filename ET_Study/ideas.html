The big bets

Three bets worth building, in depth

Big bet 1 · combines I2, I4, I11

My Portfolio: ET around what you own

Readers spend 4.1 minutes a month on ET against 8.6 on Moneycontrol, and ET's in-app price is nearly five times higher. Rivals show headlines next to holdings, but none explains what a story means for a reader's position. ET has the newsroom and ET Now to do it.

  1. A member imports holdings once, from the depository statement (CAS) they already receive.
  2. The front page becomes their stocks: stories, results and analyst notes, each with "what it means for you".
  3. On results day, a plain summary and the desk's take arrive within the hour, with a 60-second ET Now clip.
  4. Each month, a short note: what moved in their portfolio, and what ET explained about it.

Why ET can win this

A broker has the holdings but no newsroom of its own. ET has the reporting, and the depository statement lets a reader bring holdings from every broker at once, with consent. A tracker alone did not keep readers; the explanation is what they cannot get elsewhere.

How it makes money

It is the reason to pay ₹2,399 rather than ₹499 in-app, and to keep paying. Free readers can follow one holding to taste it; members follow all of them. Success shows up in weekly engaged members and renewals, not in a new price.

Cheapest first test

A trial with 5,000 members, compared with 5,000 who do not get it. Measure days active a week and renewal intent over 8 weeks. Build only the importer and the mapping; reuse existing stories.

What could go wrong

Advice rules: explaining a story for a reader's own holdings sits close to personal investment advice under SEBI's rules, so it must explain, never recommend, and pass a legal review. Data: the statement carries PAN and holdings, so import needs clear consent under India's data protection law, minimal storage and one-tap deletion.

Stop if: fewer than 15% of invited members import holdings within 30 days, or those who import are not active at least 15% more days a week than a matched group that did not get it.

Sources: What the depository statement (CAS) is · Comscore, February 2026 (Storyboard18)

Big bet 2 · combines I1, I3, I12

Renew by value: member mode, Investor Wrapped and pause

Up to 1 in 5 recent negative reviews of the ET app is about ads or upsells after paying. Renewal is where ET's subscription money is decided, and today the experience before renewal is the one most likely to make a member leave.

  1. From the day they pay, a member sees at most one ad per story, no pop-ups and no sales calls.
  2. Each month, a value receipt: stories read, tools used, ads avoided.
  3. Before renewal, Investor Wrapped: the year in stocks followed and lessons learned, made to share.
  4. At renewal, "pause for a month" sits beside "cancel", one tap each.

Why ET can win this

ET already has the reading and tool data to make a recap personal, and a recap made to share carries ET's brand into the member's own network for free.

How it makes money

On public numbers, each point of renewal is worth up to about ₹47 lakh a year (the plan page shows the working), so the aim is five points, about ₹2.4 crore. Shared recaps bring new readers at no media cost.

Cheapest first test

Give member mode, the value receipt, Investor Wrapped and pause to half of the members renewing over two quarters; the other half keep today's experience. Compare renewal rates.

What could go wrong

Ad revenue from members falls; if members read five times as much as the average visitor, they are still under 3% of page views. Recaps leave out holdings unless the member chooses to share them. A recap of a quiet year can remind a member they hardly used ET: send it above a reading threshold, and a "here is what you missed" version to the rest.

Stop if: renewal in the half that got it is not at least two points higher than in the other half, over two quarters.

Sources: ET app reviews, Google Play · Crisil rating report, June 2025

Big bet 3 · combines I5, I6, I14

Ask ET: an answer engine, and an archive that earns

Google search referrals to publishers fell 40.2% in a year, while weekly use of AI chatbots for news rose from 7% to 10% in the countries surveyed. Readers increasingly ask rather than search, and decades of Indian business reporting are what those answers need.

  1. A reader asks, on ET or WhatsApp, "What did the RBI's last policy mean for home loans?"
  2. Ask ET answers in plain words, citing the ET stories it used, with their dates.
  3. Three answers a day are free; members get unlimited answers and the Prime pieces behind them.
  4. The same archive, licensed, earns from AI companies that want trusted Indian business news.

Why ET can win this

Answers grounded in one newsroom's reporting can cite and date every claim, which general chatbots struggle to do on Indian business detail.

How it makes money

Sign-ins and Prime starts from answer users, plus licensing. Abroad, News Corp's 2024 deal with OpenAI, covering current and archived content, was reported as possibly worth over $250 million across five years, for many titles, so a ceiling rather than a comparison.

Cheapest first test

Ask ET on 10% of article pages for six weeks, answering only from ET's own stories. Measure sign-ins, answer ratings and Prime starts from answers.

What could go wrong

Wrong answers damage trust: answer only from ET text, always cite, and say "I do not know" when the archive is silent. Licensing needs a rights check first, since wire, syndicated and partner content may not be ET's to license, and it can feed the tools that take readers away.

Stop if: fewer than 2% of answer users sign in, or editors checking a weekly sample find more than 1 answer in 50 wrong.

Sources: Chartbeat, search referrals (Android Headlines) · Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026 · News Corp and OpenAI deal (PYMNTS)

Judgement

Tempting ideas I would not do, and why

  • Cut the price to ₹499 to match Moneycontrol. ET's subscription money at list price is already about the same as Moneycontrol Pro's. At ₹499, ET would need about 5.4 times its members just to keep the same money.
  • Rebuild the old portfolio tracker as it was. Holdings live at the broker. Asking readers to type them in again is the job brokers already won; importing the depository statement gives the same data and ties it to stories.
  • Put all markets news behind the paywall. Advertising is 65 to 70% of Times Internet's revenue and search traffic is falling. Closing the top of the funnel shrinks both the ad base and next year's subscribers.
  • Run more "Buy 1, get 1 free" sales. Bonus-month bundles are already the default. More of them teach buyers to wait for the next sale, and lower the price at renewal.
  • Grow the sales team. Reviewers already report calls after paying or saying no. More calls make P1 worse; better targeting raises revenue per call.

The near-term work is on the fixes page; how I would sequence it and measure it is on the plan page.
