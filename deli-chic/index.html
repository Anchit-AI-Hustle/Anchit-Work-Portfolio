Design notes · concept by Anchit Tandon

Why this storefront looks the way it does.

Deli Chic is a real Delhi NCR gourmet butcher. This page is my independent redesign of its storefront, built to show how I would carry a shopper from a first look to a confident order. It is not Deli Chic's site.

The problem

A premium butcher sells on trust and freshness, and a shopper wants to know three things fast: is it good, is it cold all the way to my door, and does it deliver to me. I had no access to Deli Chic's analytics, so everything below is a design hypothesis, not a finding.

What I kept

  • The brand's own catalogue: product names, prices and photographs from its public shop.
  • Its temperature claim, Chilled, Never Frozen, and the 0°C–4°C cold chain it describes.
  • Its four Delhi NCR hubs and two culinary lineages, European charcuterie and Awadhi marinades.

What I changed

  • A larder with filters and a basket drawer, so a shopper never leaves the page to build an order.
  • A cold-room walk-through that shows the process, cut to sealed, as the one signature moment.
  • The four hubs, the areas each covers and the two delivery slots, shown before the basket rather than after it.
  • Slower, heavier motion than my Gram Street concept, to match a cold-chain brand.

Hypothesis

If the cold chain and the delivery hubs are visible before the basket, fewer shoppers abandon at checkout over the two questions that matter for raw protein: freshness and whether it reaches them.

How I would measure it Intended, not measured

  • Add-to-basket rate from the larder.
  • Basket to order-preview completion.
  • WhatsApp order-draft starts.

This concept has no live traffic, so none of these has been measured.

What is real and what is a demo

  • From Deli Chic's public catalogue: product names, prices, product photos, and product claims such as antibiotic residue-free. I have not verified those claims.
  • Demo: the basket and checkout (no card is taken; WhatsApp only drafts a message marked DEMO), the promo code and the hub status line.
  • Illustrative: the atmosphere photography. Phone and WhatsApp links go to the brand, not to me.

How checkout works here

The order is simulated end to end: you get a preview and a WhatsApp draft you can read, and nothing is sent. In my Xazina concept the bag hands off to Xazina's real cart instead, because that catalogue is a real snapshot of the live store.

Motion decisions

  • No motion on raw-protein imagery: a pulse or a warm colour cycle on a cut reads as spoilage.
  • No springy or bouncy easing anywhere; it contradicts the cold-chain claim.
  • One pinned scroll-through, and everything decorative pauses off screen.
