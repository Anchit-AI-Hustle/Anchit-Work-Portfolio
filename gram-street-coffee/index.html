From pie shop to purple latte.

Chef Vanshika Bhatia built Gram Street as a compact, beverage-first counter, and she keeps building focused culinary destinations.

  1. 2021Petite Pie Shop. A pie-led kitchen that became a local culinary sensation.
  2. 2022OMO. An ingredient-forward plant-based kitchen launched with Bright Hospitality.
  3. NowGram Street Coffee. Specialty coffee, ceremonial matcha and small-batch gelato in a room made for conversation.

Lilac glass. Chrome accents. A tiny room by design.

A glass front painted lilac, chrome cutting through the pastel interior, and a design philosophy drawn from contemporary Asian specialty bars. Bhatia deliberately avoided turning it into a remote workstation: there is just enough room to savor an espresso, and that is the entire point.

Behind the drinks sit curated sandos on ciabatta and Japanese milk bread, such as the eggplant parm and jerk chicken, alongside the blueberry brioche and flaky K-Drama puff pastry.

A movement, city by city.

Gram Street began in Gurugram across DLF Phase 1, Golf Course Road and Sector 56. Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Jaipur are the planned next destinations, with Dubai, Singapore and London thereafter.

Design notes · concept by Anchit Tandon

Why this storefront looks the way it does.

Gram Street Coffee is a real Gurugram café. This page is my independent redesign of its storefront, built to show how I would take a first-time visitor from curiosity to an order. It is not the café's site.

The problem

A café site has two jobs for a first-time visitor: show what the place is in one screen, then get them to an order or to the counter. I had no access to Gram Street's analytics, so everything below is a design hypothesis, not a finding.

What I kept

  • The brand's own material language: lilac glass, chrome, a tiny room by design.
  • Its three pillars, in its order: coffee, matcha, gelato.
  • The three Gurugram counters, and the real Zomato, Swiggy and Instagram links.

What I changed

  • A walk-in scroll from the street to the counter, as the one signature moment.
  • A menu with a bag and a two-step checkout that keeps a recap of the order.
  • An outlet finder with a tab per counter, which hides an order link that would have pointed to the wrong outlet.
  • One primary call to action in the hero: the shop.

Hypothesis

If the menu and the nearest counter are each one tap from the first screen, more first-time visitors reach an order or directions than on a page that leads with the story.

How I would measure it Intended, not measured

  • Hero to menu click rate.
  • Add-to-bag rate, and bag to checkout completion.
  • Directions taps per outlet.

This concept has no live traffic, so none of these has been measured.

What is real and what is a demo

  • Real: the outbound Instagram, Zomato and Swiggy links.
  • Demo: the bag and checkout (no card is taken; WhatsApp only drafts a message marked DEMO), the guestlist and RSVPs (nothing is stored) and the bar status line.
  • Illustrative: the photography, the founder story and the city plans.

How checkout works here

The order is simulated end to end: you get a reference and a WhatsApp draft you can read, and nothing leaves the page. In my Xazina concept the bag hands off to Xazina's real cart instead, because that catalogue is a real snapshot of the live store.

Motion decisions

  • One pinned scroll-through, never a second one competing with it.
  • No cursor trail, confetti or particle bursts: the brand's claim is precision.
  • Everything decorative pauses off screen and under reduced motion.
